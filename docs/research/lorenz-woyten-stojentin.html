von Woyten og von Stojentin: identiteter og afstamningspåstande

Foreløbig hypotese

Claim

De indsendte og publicerede pedigree-spor forbinder von Woyten- og von Stojentin-navne med Kleist-linjen, men flere personer med samme navn og de middelalderlige afstamningspåstande er ikke sikkert identificeret. To Georg-personer i Christina Woyten-sporet skal holdes adskilt.

Evidence

Lorenz-dossieret sammenholder Harbou, den nyere von-Kleist-familiehistorie, Deutsche Biographie og Mörres studie af Swenzonerne; materialet afviser nogle af de leverede Puttkamer-/Swenzone-forbindelser som utilstrækkeligt belagte.

Missing

Præcise arkivhenvisninger til de enkelte Woyten/Stojentin-personer, original dåb/vielse/skifte og sikker identifikation af Christina Woytens far.

Next

Kontrollér Christina Woytens to mulige Georg-fædre i samtidige kirkebøger og skifter; efterprøv derefter hver ældre Woyten/Stojentin-generation mod originaldokumenter og moderne forskning.

Note: 1. Verified von Woyten / von Kleist additions

The English translation of the von Kleist family history, History of the Family von Kleist – Tychow-Dubberow line, p. 123, states that:

  • Georg von Kleist of Groß-Tychow (died after 1645) was married to Christina von Woyten.
  • Christina was the widow of Otto von Below of Pustamin before marrying Georg.
  • Christina was the daughter of Benedictus von Woyten of Versin, princely Pomeranian captain of Bütow, and Elisabeth von Stojentin of Giesebitz.
  • Georg and Christina had one daughter, Hedwig Maria, who married Colonel Christian von Kleist (II. 60).
  • Georg's feudal estates passed to his son-in-law Christian.

The structured data includes Benedictus, Elisabeth and Otto, and records Christina's parents and her marriage to Georg. The first marriage to Otto is a separate union, not biological parentage.

Source: von Kleist Family Archive, English translation, p. 123.

Note: G. Collateral Woyten and Stojentin leads

These names are relevant to the wider family network but do not extend the medieval von Kleist male line:

  • Christina von Woyten: the 2025 Tychow-Dubberow volume says she was the widow of Otto von Below of Pustamin, daughter of Benedictus von Woyten of Versin and Elisabeth von Stojentin of Giesebitz, and wife of Georg von Kleist. It identifies Hedwig Maria as Georg and Christina's only daughter/only legitimate heir in the passage. This supports the named marriage/parentage claims as a published family-history account; recheck the German text and cited records before adding collateral children.
  • Earlier von Woyten sequence: the archived ledger proposes Michael → Conrad → Conrad Pantaleon → Vandemar → Carl Woiten (flourishing c. 1400), with Martin von Woyten as a great-uncle of Benedictus. No precise page/column references or transcriptions were supplied. Unverified lead only. The Zedler vol. 59 “Woyden/Woyten” article must be located and transcribed before any links are accepted.
  • Earlier von Stojentin sequence: Hans/Johann von Stojentin (1446–1517) is proposed as father of Elisabeth of Giesebitz, with Peter von Stojentin and Elisabeth von Lettow as his parents. The dossier itself calls this probable, and the estate association with Giesebitz is not parentage evidence. Hypothesis only.
  • Otto von Below is Christina's earlier spouse; do not make him Hedwig Maria's father.

Note: What the 2025 von Kleist family history confirms

The 2025 revised Tychow-Dubberow history (English machine translation; German edition 2024) gives a strong published-genealogy framework:

  1. Georg II.55 and Christina: Georg is recorded as still a minor in 1609, taxed at Groß-Tychow in 1629, and holding shares in Mandelatz and Groß-Tychow in 1645. The compiler infers that he died after 1645 and certainly before 1665. His wife is named Christina von Woyten, widow of Otto von Below of Pustamin, daughter of Benedictus von Woyten of Versin and Elisabeth von Stojentin of Giesebitz. Their only daughter is Hedwig Maria. No birth or death date is supplied for Christina or Hedwig Maria. The book does not support estimating Christina's dates from her father's lifespan.
  2. Hedwig Maria and Christian/Carsten II.60: the colonel died in 1679. His first marriage to Hedwig Maria produced four sons — Bernd Erdmann II.81, Christian Casimir II.82, Adam Wilhelm II.83 and Sebastian Heinrich II.84 — and daughter Herath Juliana. His second marriage, to Barbara Eleonora von Kalkstein, produced a separate son and daughter. Keep the two marriages' children distinct.
  3. Bernd Erdmann II.81: died 26 December 1701 at Helsingør and was buried at St. Mary's Church. His wife was Elisabeth Hedwig von Schack. They had son Christian Wilhelm II.116 and four daughters: Juliana Loysa, Maria Elisabeth, Amalia and Charlotta.
  4. Christian Wilhelm II.116: born circa 1684 and died 1763. He married Anna Elisabeth von Kleist and Agnes Maria von Kleist. The book lists five sons — Bernd Dubslaff II.153, Ernst Ewald II.154, Rüdiger Christian II.155, Hans Sigismund II.156 and Joachim (Rüdiger) Wilhelm II.157 — and daughter Hedwig Elisabeth from the second marriage. The five sons' branch is described as ending with their childless deaths. Some individual dates in the supplied compilation need correction: for example, II.153 is given as born circa 1718 and died 1769 in the biography, but the same entry's narrative says he died 22 November 1756; this internal inconsistency needs checking against the cited source/records before reusing either death year.
  5. Christian Casimir II.82: born 1654, died 19 February 1722. His wife is named Anna Hedwig von Fürst, who died in 1722. The couple had one son, Andreas Wilhelm II.117, and four daughters, including Barbara Juliane, who married Lieutenant Colonel Cartz Ulrik von Kleist III.369. This is the crucial bridge to Christian Adam: he is not a son of Christian Casimir. The descent runs through Christian Casimir → Barbara Juliane → her marriage to Cartz Ulrik → Christian Adam III.466. The supplied list incorrectly places Barbara Juliane and Andreas Wilhelm as children of Christian Adam in its Danish-branch section; they are siblings/children of Christian Casimir in the earlier generation. The book says the male branch through Andreas Wilhelm later died out with his grandchildren; that does not erase the separate descent through Barbara Juliane.

Note: The Christina Woyten identity question: two Georgs

The same 2025 book preserves the unresolved point already noted in the canonical record: - Georg II.55 of Groß-Tychow (died after 1645) had a wife Christina von Woyten, widow of Otto von Below, and daughter Hedwig Maria. - Georg II.44 of Wendisch-Tychow/Crolow died in 1629. The book reports, following von der Osten, a first wife Christina Woyten of Versin, said to have borne him no children, and a second wife Dorothea von Below, who bore two sons. The compiler says other family records do not mention II.44's first wife. A note attached to the II.55 account says von der Osten mistakenly assigns II.55's Christina to Georg II.44.

This is not proof that one woman married both Georgs, nor proof that there were two women. The second account is explicitly dependent on von der Osten and is absent from other family records. A possible sequence involving II.44, Otto von Below and II.55 remains a hypothesis only. Do not merge identities, change Christina's spouse links, or create a second Christina node until the original cited source behind von der Osten and the relevant estate/marriage records are inspected.

Note: The Swenzone / Puttkamer ancestry: a significant correction

The supplied account presents Anna von Puttkamer's family as reaching back to the Swenzone dynasty around 1257–1260. This is not a secure genealogical connection.

  • The Deutsche Biographie states that the Puttkamer name derives from the Polish office title podkomorzy (under-chamberlain), that the family is attested in the Stolp area in 1257, but that the proposed genealogical relationship to the Swenzones is unclear and modern scholarship is sceptical. It explicitly says the family is now considered probably immigrant rather than autochthonous Pomeranian, while its ethnic classification remains open.
  • Fritz Mörre's 1939 study, Die Swenzonen in Ostpommern, reviews the old claimed descent and concludes that there is no proof of a direct continuation from the Swenzones into the Puttkamer family. He explains that the 1425 "Lorenz Swenz genannt Puttkamer" was likely a member of the Swenz family, not a Puttkamer, and that Peter Putkummer appears as a vassal/witness in Swenzone documents rather than a proven relative.
  • Therefore, the supplied statement that the Puttkamer line is demonstrably descended from Swenzo should be removed from any factual narrative. It may be mentioned only as an older, disputed family tradition. The Puttkamer family itself is historically documented in Pomerania; that does not prove Anna von Puttkamer's exact parentage or connect her to Martin von Woyten.

Note: Stojentin and Woyten medieval origins

  • Valentin von Stojentin is independently documented as a Pomeranian councillor/court official, born around 1485 and dying in 1528 or 1529. This establishes a historical Stojentin figure, not that he was Christina's maternal grandfather or a direct ancestor of Elisabeth von Stojentin. The exact Giesebitz line still needs estate and church evidence.
  • Bagmihl's Pommersches Wappenbuch is a heraldic compilation. A coat of arms or a list of estates can help identify a family, but cannot by itself prove a parent-child relationship, the alleged Woyten etymology from wojt/Wojciech, or a descent from medieval Slavic nobility. The linguistic and ethnic claims in the supplied overview remain hypotheses pending specialist onomastic and archival sources.
  • The claims that Martin von Woyten held Varsin, Wessin, Alt Reitz and Vigelow, and that Benedictus von Woyten held Versin and served as a ducal captain/official at Bütow, remain useful leads. They still need exact references to contemporary feudal registers, land records or the relevant pages of a critical family history. Do not treat the proposed Martin → Benedictus father-son link or Anna von Puttkamer's parentage as established solely from the supplied pedigree.

Note: 3. von Stojentin maternal ancestry — possible, not established

The dossier suggests Hans (Johann) von Stojentin (1446–1517), lord of Gohren and Landvogt of Stolp, may have been Elisabeth von Stojentin of Giesebitz's father. It further names Peter von Stojentin (c. 1400–1470) and Elisabeth von Lettow as Hans's parents.

The dossier itself describes the connection to Elisabeth of Giesebitz as probable rather than demonstrated. An estate association with Giesebitz is not proof of parentage. No link should be added until the identity and relationship are corroborated.

Note: 6.4 Christina von Woyten — possible collateral children

The expanded dossier describes Hedwig Maria and “collateral siblings” as Christina's children. This conflicts with the von Kleist family archive translation, p. 123, which identifies Hedwig Maria as Georg and Christina's only legitimate child. The word “legitimate” leaves a research question about any other biological children, but does not prove that such children existed.

Search the cited Zedler article and contemporary church, probate or estate records for individually named children and explicit parentage. Add any well-supported non-marital children without excluding them because of status, but do not manufacture siblings from a general phrase.
