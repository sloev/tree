## 1. Three contemporary royal-chancery notices naming Knud Giedde in 1657

The published calendar *Kancelliets Brevbøger vedrørende Danmarks indre forhold* (1657) contains three distinct notices:

- **31 January 1657:** Knud Giedde is authorised to recruit 50 men in Lund for his company.
- **16 February 1657:** Ove Giedde receives a letter concerning Knud Giedde serving under his company and the prescribed horses for the clergy and bailiff in Ove's fief. A second letter the same day authorises Ove to recruit 50 young men for **Christoffer Giedde** to establish his own company in Helsingborg len.
- **26 March 1657:** Knud Giedde is granted six to eight weeks' leave to travel to Norway for his sister's funeral.

The printed calendar cites original Chancellery/Skånske Tegnelser references. The 26 March entry is especially distinctive: it establishes a named sister in Norway, although it does not name her. The 1657 Knud is almost certainly **Knud Giedde, son of Ove Giedde, who died in 1707**, rather than the separate Major Knud (d.1658) in the later officer-family genealogy. Crucially, the biographical dictionary *Eneveldets menn i Norge* (2000), p.175, says Ove's son Knud entered military service as an officer in 1657 and became major from 1659 — an exact career match to the Chancellery notices. The 26 March leave for his sister's funeral is a striking match for **Regitze Ovesdatter Giedde**, daughter of Ove and Dorthe Urne, who is independently recorded as dying in Bergen on 1 March 1657. The same 2000 biographical dictionary identifies Ove Bjelke's wife Regitze Giedde as a daughter of Ove and Dorthe, married in 1647 and deceased in Bergen in 1657. SNL independently lists Regitze (1629–1657) and Christoffer (1637–1705) among Ove's children, and notes Christoffer's Swedish ennoblement as Gädda in 1664. The Chancellery letter does not name the sister, so the identification as Regitze is still indirect, but date, place, parentage and the 2000 biographical record align exceptionally closely.

## 2. The oldest direct ancestor: Christian Gedde and his father Major Knud

The line through Samuel Christopher Gedde (1691–1766) can now be traced to his father Christian Gedde and, through a family letter of 1782, to Major Knud Gedde. A. Thiset's 1882 article quotes the letter written by Generalmajor Hans Christopher Gedde to genealogist Bertouch on 17 February 1782. It says Christian died in 1707 aged 59, was married to Charlotte von Geidem from Courland, and was the son of Major Knud, who fell in the Ulfeldt War. This is the strongest evidence for the direct parent-child link, although the original letter in the Geheimearkiv's genealogical-heraldic archive still needs inspection.

Other biographical sources give differing dates and details:

- **Dansk biografisk Lexikon (1887):** identifies Samuel Christopher's father as Christian Gedde, described as colonel and commandant on Møn, born 1648 and died 1707, married to Charlotte von Geidern.
- **Anker, Biografiske data om 330 norske... (1885):** gives a Christian Gedde born about 1646, died 2 May 1705 aged 59, son of Major Knud Gedde, and married to Charlotte von Geidern.
- **Norske Slægter (1912), p. 128:** describes an officer-family ancestor Major Knud Giedde (d. 1658) and his son, ritmester Christian Giedde (d. 1705), father of Samuel Christopher.
- **Susanne Andersen, Beboere i Damsholte Distrikt på Møn (2022):** lists Christian Gedde as 1639–1705, born in Bohuslän, son of Major Knud Gedde; it says a 1693 Stambog recorded him as 54 years old, married and father of three sons and one daughter. The compiler explicitly warns that pre-1660 birth years are calculated from reported ages at death and may be wrong.

The 2022 compilation links to a FamilySearch image for the Stambog entry:
https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-C33K-LFZM-Y?i=24&cat=416356

**Interpretation:** Christian is the earliest currently supported direct ancestor before Samuel Christopher. The 1782 family letter, as quoted by Thiset, supports death in 1707 aged 59 (birth c.1648) and father Major Knud; Anker and DBL agree on the 1648–1707 range. The 1693 Stambog transcription (age 54 in 1693, implying c.1639) conflicts and must be checked against the original. The strongest next target is the original 1782 letter, followed by the 1693 muster roll.

## 3. The old noble Gjedde connection: explicit refutation and later confusion

*Danmarks Adels Aarbog* (1894), p. 123, addresses the claim that the officer-family ancestor Ritmester Christian Giedde (d. 1705) was a nephew of Rigsadmiral Ove Giedde. It states that the claim's “absolute incorrectness” had been demonstrated elsewhere, and that no other connection between the officer family and the noble Giedde family had been shown and was not likely.

Source: https://runeberg.org/adelaarbog/1894/0167.html

*Norske Slægter* (1912), p. 128, later says that Major Knud Giedde (d. 1658) had previously been considered identical with Ove Giedde's elder brother Knud, while describing that relationship as disputed. The date for Ove's brother in the 1894 noble-family genealogy is reported as before 30 July 1612 (DAA 1894, p. 124, cited in the genealogical index). If that date is confirmed in the original page, the elder brother cannot be the Knud active in 1657 or a Major Knud who died in 1658.

A separate Knud must not be confused with either of these men: the Riksarkivaren biographical dictionary *Eneveldets menn i Norge* (2000), p. 175, identifies Knud Giedde (d. 1707) as a son of Ove Giedde and an amtmann in Nordland and Vardøhus. This is a distinct person from a putative Major Knud d. 1658.

The 1928 *Personalhistorisk Tidsskrift* and modern Store norske leksikon identify **Christoffer Giedde (1637–1705)** as another son of Ove and Dorthe, a major who was naturalised as a Swedish nobleman under the name **Gädda** in 1664. The 16 February 1657 company-raising letter is an exact military-context match for this Christoffer. The 1657 Knud is almost certainly Ove's son Knud (d.1707), not the separate Major Knud d.1658 in the civil officer-family account. These repeated names and titles make identity conflation a serious risk.

## 4. Modern expert assessment and the 1950 Brenner pedigree

Store norske leksikon's article “Gedde”, written by conservator Terje Bratberg and last updated 25 November 2024, says the Norwegian Gedde family probably has no connection with the extinct Danish-Norwegian noble Gjedde family:
https://snl.no/Gedde

The article's comment thread is particularly relevant. A descendant stated in 2013 that a 1950 family tree made by Personalhistorisk Institut in Copenhagen began with “Nils Gädda” around 1370 and included a side branch containing Ove Giedde, while the descendant's direct line began with Samuel Christopher Gedde. On 5 January 2014, SNL's editor Jon Gunnar Arntzen replied that the tree had been produced by a Copenhagen firm known for poor and partly fabricated genealogical source material, and that it should be treated with the utmost caution and skepticism.

A separate 2013 blog post by a descendant of the noble Ove Giedde line says Carlo Octavius Gede (born 1895) commissioned a Gedde/Gede pedigree in 1944 from S. Otto Brenner; the work was never completed, and a letter from H.H. Worsøe allegedly warned Carlo about Brenner. This account is a lead only; the letter and original tree have not been independently located:
https://ove-gedde.blogspot.com/2013/05/ove-geddes-dorthe-urnes-children.html

## Evidence conclusion

- The direct officer-family line through Samuel Christopher and Hans Christopher is supported by separate biographical and cartographic sources.
- Christian Gedde is a strong candidate for the earlier direct ancestor; his dates and parentage are conflicting.
- A Knud Giedde was demonstrably active in royal military administration in 1657 and travelled to Norway for his sister's funeral. Cross-referencing his service under Ove Giedde with the 1707 biography of Ove's son Knud strongly identifies him as that son; the sister is very likely Regitze Ovesdatter Gedde, who died in Bergen on 1 March 1657.
- The claimed nephew relationship between Christian Gedde and Ove Giedde was explicitly rejected in *Danmarks Adels Aarbog* (1894); the modern SNL assessment also says the families are probably unrelated.
- The 1950 Personalhistorisk Institut tree is not admissible as proof of medieval descent without independent original sources.
