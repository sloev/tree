## Photographic object evidence

Barbara Świadek's article in *Krzysztofory* 41 (2023), pp.43–62, studies a silver-gilt chalice held by the Museum of Kraków since 1975, inventory **MHK-1109/II**. On printed p.45, figures 2–5 reproduce photographs credited to Bogdan Krężel:

- Fig. 2: the chalice foot.
- Fig. 3: the von Zarten coat of arms.
- Fig. 4: the von Blankenburg coat of arms.
- Fig. 5: the inscription on the chalice foot.

The inscription reads **“ANNA BLANKENBORGES”** and beneath it **“E·L·Z·S·N WITWE”**. The visual evidence therefore associates the object with a widow named Anna Blankenburg and displays both Zarten and Blankenburg heraldry.

## What the article concludes — and does not conclude

The article interprets the object as a foundation by Anna Blankenburg-Zarten, but on printed p.51 it explicitly states that the sparse evidence cannot establish whether this Anna was the wife of the Ernst Zarten listed in the Cammin tax register. It considers two possibilities: Anna's maiden name may have been Blankenburg and Zarten her married name, or Anna may have been born Blankenburg-Zarten and the Z in the inscription may refer to her birth family. The proposed expansion of the initials E·L·Z·S·N as a deceased Zarten husband's initials is explicitly conjectural.

A separate 1588 report states that Hans von Zarten sold his son Ernst rights to estates at Glodowa, Dubbertech and Priddargen. The 17th-century tax register lists Ernst Zarten at Priddargen and Dubbertech. These records make the Ernst identification a valuable research hypothesis, but do not prove that Anna was his wife.
