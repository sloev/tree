## Relevant text and findings

The searchable PDF identifies **Friderik Ernst Fristedt Fischer**, born 20 October 1846 and baptised 24 November 1846 at Fuur. On printed p.23 Arends states that Fischer attended Odense Latinskole, abandoned his studies, went as a young man to the Cape Colony, and worked in the diamond mines at Kimberley. Arends adds that he was *antagelig* dead there and *formentlig* unmarried. These qualifiers mean that neither death at Kimberley nor unmarried status is established.

The same genealogy supplies a Gedde connection:
- Printed p.17: Caroline Frideriche Wilhelmine Gedde (1775–1818), daughter of Captain/later Major-General Salomon Gedde and Catharina Elisabeth Qvist, married Jens Henrich Fischer on 19 March 1796.
- Printed pp.22–23: their descendant Ernst Christian Fischer (1806–1878) had daughter Ingertha Cathrine Jensine Fischer (1842–1922), who married Carl Vilhelm Ferdinand Arends on 7 October 1863. Their son Otto Frederik Arends, born 12 June 1864, is the author.
- Printed p.3: Arends warns that he had not obtained exhaustive information on descendants living abroad and sometimes relied on second-hand information.

## Evidentiary value

This is a specific, contemporary-to-the-family genealogical account of a Gedde-descended Fischer who went to South Africa and worked at Kimberley. It supports a **reported Cape Colony / Kimberley migration destination and workplace**, but not a complete travel route. No departure date, ship, arrival port, mine employer, death date, burial place, or original South African record is supplied.
