## Note: What the civil records say

The Egernsund civil birth register, 1932 no. 4 (BSID 37658, image 125), records that Else Gedde Frederiksen, née Jensen, gave birth to Lorenz on 12 January 1932 at 05:30. A marginal note dated 11 July 1932 reportedly names landworker Wilhelm Krogh of Snogbæk as father, based on:
- a paternity declaration dated 5 April 1932;
- a court decision dated 10 June 1932.

The Broager Vestre baptism register, 1933 no. 1 (BSID 201141, image 112), names worker Peter Frederiksen and wife Else Gedde Jensen as parents and refers to a letter from the civil registrar dated 11 July 1932. Peter was Lorenz's social/stepfather and gave him his surname.

These sources establish what was officially declared/registered and what the church register recorded. They do not, without the underlying paternity case and further evidence, conclusively establish biological paternity.

THE DANISH PATERNITY LAW OF 1932 — LEGAL CONTEXT
==============================================================================

In 1932, Aabenraa-Sønderborg Amt applied Danish paternity law (since
the area had been returned to Denmark in 1920 after the plebiscite).

Key legal principles:
  • An unmarried mother could name the alleged father.
  • The alleged father could acknowledge paternity voluntarily
    (faderskabserklæring) — this is what Wilhelm did on 5 April 1932.
  • If the alleged father denied paternity, the case went to civil court
    (retssag), where evidence (witnesses, correspondence) was weighed.
  • If the court found paternity likely, it issued a "retsafgørelse"
    ordering the man to pay child support (alimentation).
  • Paternity could be assigned even without biological certainty if
    the evidence was strong enough.

The 10 June 1932 court decision in Lorenz's case could be either:
  • Uncontested confirmation of Wilhelm's voluntary declaration, OR
  • Contested litigation that Wilhelm lost.

Without reading the actual case file, we cannot distinguish. The case
file is at:
  Landsarkivet for Sønderjylland, Aabenraa
  Archive: Aabenraa Amt 1920-1932 / Aabenraa-Sønderborg Amt 1932-1972
  Series: Alimentationsjournal 1932 (or Almindelig Journal 1932, or
          Journalsager / Alimentationssager)
  Access: 75-year closure period has expired (1932 + 75 = 2007).
  Finding aid: Unprinted, in reading room at Aabenraa.

To request access:
  Contact: Landsarkivet for Sønderjylland, Aabenraa
  Website: https://www.rigsarkivet.dk/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/Landsarkivet-for-Soenderjylland-En-arkivoversigt.pdf
  Guide: https://www.rigsarkivet.dk/vejledning/faderskab-og-adoption/faderskabssager-kom-godt-i-gang/

## Note: Official archival procedure verified for 1919–1970 cases

Rigsarkivet's current guide states that from 1919 to 1970 paternity cases began with police notification or voluntary acknowledgement; if paternity was disputed, civil court decided it, and the county's alimentations case file often also contains the civil court paternity case. The recommended search order is county archive first (register to alimentations journal → journal → case file), then civil court case lists/judgment books, then police records. This materially sharpens the existing Aabenraa-Sønderborg Amt 1932 lead: search the 1932 county archive's register/alimentations journal and request the linked case file, then use civil court records only if the county file is incomplete. The 75-year access period has expired for a 1932 file. This official guidance confirms the route, not the contents or outcome of Lorenz's particular case.

## Note: Rigsarkivets officielle søgevej for faderskabssager 1919–1970

Rigsarkivets aktuelle vejledning fastslår, at sager fra 1919–1970 typisk først bør søges i amtets arkiv: find register til alimentationsjournal og alimentationsjournal, slå moderens eller faderens navn op, og brug journalnummeret til at bestille den tilhørende alimentations-/journalsag. Hvis sagen ikke findes i amtsarkivet, er næste spor civilrettens saglister/dombøger og derefter politiets faderskabsprotokoller. Vejledningen siger, at journalsagerne ikke er skannet og skal bestilles til læsesalen. For 1932-sagen er det derfor rigtigt at begynde med Sønderborg Amt (og kontrollere Aabenraa-Sønderborg Amts arkivstruktur), men man skal først finde journalregisterets konkrete opslag. Den offentlige vejledning dokumenterer søgemetoden, ikke at Lorenz' sag er fundet.
