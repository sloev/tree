## Claim

Den publicerede Gedde-officerslægts litteratur rummer flere forhold, der kræver kontrol: Christian Gedde-navnebrødre, Samuel Christophs børnegruppe, Fischer-søstrenes adskilte identiteter og Nicolai Wilhelm Geddes modstridende dødsdatoer i 1833. De er kilde- og identitetsspørgsmål, ikke nye dokumenterede slægtsled.

## Note: Christian Giedde / Christian Gedde — possible namesake conflation

Two incompatible source descriptions exist for the earlier Christian Gedde/Giedde said to be father of Samuel Christoph Gedde (`p864`):

- The DBL / Harbou-derived source used in the earlier audit identifies **Christian Gedde (1648–1707)**, colonel and commandant on Møn, husband of Charlotte von Geidern, as father of Samuel Christoph (born 14 July 1691).
- The separate evidence-audit compilation cites Danish Wikipedia for **Christian Giedde (born 1639 in Kville, died 1705)**, husband of Anna Margrethe Charlotte von Geijder (born 1648 on Øsel; daughter of Carl Christian von Geijder and Charlotte von Vietinghoff), and recommends changing p864's father's birth year from 1638 to 1639.

These cannot be silently merged: the birth/death years and wife's name differ. The 1639 correction may apply to the person currently encoded as p864's father, while the DBL 1648–1707 Møn commandant may be a different Christian or a source conflation. Reconcile the original DBL article, Harbou, Schøller's officer-family genealogy and Møn/Kville records before changing p864's father's dates, spouse or identity. Search Kville baptisms and Møn commandant records. The current 1638 date is not secure; neither is a merge with the 1648–1707 person.

## Note: The documented Danish descent

H. W. Harbou's 1897 article, “Slægten von Kleist i Danmark,” gives the following chain:
1. **Pribislaff III.281** of Muttrin, Borntin, Döbel and Drenow, and Esther von Kameke, were the parents of **Cartz Ulrik III.369**. Harbou records his Brandenburg service in Brabant (1692–1694), Danish service from 9 August 1701, promotion to lieutenant-colonel, death at Rendsburg in 1722 and burial on 17 November.
2. Cartz Ulrik married **Barbara Juliane von Kleist**, daughter of Christian Casimir II.82 of the Tychow line. This is a marriage linking the Muttrin and Tychow-Dubberow branches of the same wider family.
3. Their son **Christian Adam III.466** was born 1 October 1705 and baptised at Frelsers Kirke, Christianshavn.
4. Christian Adam's son **Christian Frederik III.601** was born 25 August 1743. He married Anna Margrethe Schubart in 1770.
5. Their daughter **Frederikke Louise Dorthea** was born 5 January 1786 and married **Ove Samuel Gedde** on 24 June 1806.

This is the explicit published bridge from the Kleist family into the Danish Gedde line. Harbou's article cites military, church, probate and official records in its notes, but remains a 1897 synthesis; the cited original records should be checked before describing every link as primary-record proven. The key names, dates and relationship statements are recorded in Harbou, pp. 95–102. Source: https://www.v-kleist.com/FG_allg/Kleist_in_Daenemark.pdf.

## Note: Keep the early-medieval uncertainty in its proper place

The 1477 charter conflict over whether Reimer and Bartes were sons of Pribbeslaw of Muttrin or Hans of Tychow concerns the collateral Tychow branch. It does not erase Harbou's later documented marriage link between Barbara Juliane of Tychow and Cartz Ulrik of Muttrin, nor the explicit Danish descent through Christian Adam, Christian Frederik and Frederikke Louise. It does, however, mean that the early Tychow pedigree must remain qualified. The 1364 Prissebur record, the 1477 collective fief letter and the later family genealogies are separate pieces of evidence; none alone proves an unbroken descent from the 13th century to Cartz Ulrik.

**Working conclusion:** the Kleist-to-Gedde connection through Cartz Ulrik and Frederikke Louise Dorthea is a credible, specifically documented published genealogy. The immediate parentage of Cartz Ulrik is supported by Harbou and the family register; the generations behind Pribislaff III.281 are supported by a denser but still secondary property/genealogy trail. The next high-value action is to retrieve the fief extracts and inheritance documents at Greifswald, then compare Harbou's Danish citations with the original church and probate records. No canonical parent edge is changed by this review.

## Note: Kleist–Gedde line: source-critical update (16 November 2026)

Harbou's 1897 account, pp. 100–102, explicitly places Christian Adam III.466 as the son of Cartz Ulrik III.369 and Barbara Juliane von Kleist, and identifies Cartz Ulrik as son of Pribislaff III.281 and Esther von Kameke. Harbou also flags the older *Dansk biografisk Lexikon* IX, p. 219, error that makes Christian Adam the son of his maternal grandfather Christian Casimir. Christian Casimir was Barbara Juliane's father and Christian Adam's maternal grandfather, not his father. Do not restore that mistaken parent edge.

The name should be normalized as **Christian Adam**, while retaining **Christian Adrian** as a historical variant in older German family literature. The von Kleist family archive's online correction reports that a Danish archival investigation established the 1 October 1705 birth date and the Adam form; this remains a reported archival finding until the underlying register entry is inspected.

**Primary-record target — Vor Frelsers, Christianshavn:** Harbou reports baptism on 1 October 1705. Search the parish minister's register FVD 1686–1722 around 1–3 October 1705 (the alternative 3 October date occurs in the older biographical account). Also request the bell-ringer's parallel baptism index, volume 20 (birth index 1704–1822), and follow its reference into the bell-ringer's baptism books, volumes 15–19 or 36. The index/catalogue identifies a search route; it is not itself proof of the parentage. The decisive check is the original baptism entry naming the parents.

**Independent identification of Frederikke:** the Fredensborg Slotssogn baptism of Ove Frederik Christopher Gedde, 24 July 1808 (BSID 594366, image 65), records his parents as Ove Samuel Gedde and “Frederikke Lovise Birthe født Kleist”. Harbou (1897) calls Ove Samuel's wife Frederikke Louise Dorthea. The matching husband, surname and chronology strongly support that this is the same wife, but preserve Birthe/Dorthea as a source variant until the original image and marriage/baptism records are compared. This baptism independently supports her identity as a née Kleist; it does not independently prove her parents.

Harbou gives Christian Frederik III.601's birth as 25 August 1743, his marriage to Anna Margrethe Schubart on 3 December 1770, and daughter Frederikke's birth as 5 January 1786. Rendsburg St. Marien and Christkirche are both candidate baptism series for 1743; neither parish is yet confirmed. Bredstedt is only a first search hypothesis for the 1770 marriage and 1786 baptism, not an established event locality. No individual entry for these events has yet been inspected.

**Current assessment:** the published descent Cartz Ulrik → Christian Adam → Christian Frederik → Frederikke → Ove Samuel Gedde is a credible, well-defined line for public display with an explicit “needs more evidence” status at the primary-register level. The 1808 baptism provides independent evidence for Ove Samuel's wife being née Kleist, but not for her parentage. No canonical relationship or confidence field is changed by this note.

Sources: [Harbou, *Slægten von Kleist i Danmark* (1897), pp. 100–102](https://www.v-kleist.com/FG_allg/Kleist_in_Daenemark.pdf); [2025 Tychow-Dubberow family history, pp. 165–166](https://www.v-kleist.com/FG/Dubberow.pdf); [Vor Frelsers church-book inventory](https://slaegtsbibliotek.dk/900379.pdf); [von Kleist family archive correction on Christian Adam](https://v-kleist.com/FG/Muttrin/fg0283a.htm); [Rigsarkivet's church-book release notes (2025)](https://www.rigsarkivet.dk/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/Nyt-fra-de-frivillige-januar-2025.pdf).
