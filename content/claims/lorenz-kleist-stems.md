## Claim

Forskningsmaterialet rummer to forskellige von Kleist-slægtsforløb og modstridende ældre rekonstruktioner. De må ikke forbindes alene på grund af fælles navn eller en foreslået middelalderlig stamfar.

## Evidence

Dossierets kildekritiske gennemgang sammenholder Harbou, 2025-familiehistorien og Muttrin-materialets dokumenter. Den fremhæver bl.a. en konflikt mellem Reimer- og Bartes-traditionerne og forskellige personer med navnet Pribislaff.

## Note: F. The separate Tychow-Dubberow → Danish Christian line

Do not splice this line into the Borntin/Muttrin sequence:

- The 2025 Tychow-Dubberow volume states that Georg II.55 of Groß-Tychow had one daughter, Hedwig Maria, who married Colonel Christian II.60; Georg died after 1645 and before 1665 and left no legitimate heirs. The same biography identifies Christina von Woyten as Georg's wife and her parents as Benedictus von Woyten and Elisabeth von Stojentin. Source: [Tychow-Dubberow English translation](https://v-kleist.com/FG/english/Dubberow_translation.pdf), PDF text around lines 3074–3075.
- Harbou says Christian II.60 inherited an estate share from his father and another share through Hedwig Maria, but does not name the father in that paragraph. The 2025 Tychow-Dubberow genealogy gives the line **Bartes II.3 → Andreas/Drews II.11 → Carsten II.21 → Bernd II.39 → Christian II.60**.
- The father of Reimer and Bartes remains disputed as set out above. That conflict affects the earliest Tychow bridge, not the later Bartes → Andreas → Carsten → Bernd → Christian sequence.
- The 2025 Tychow-Dubberow volume is a revised family genealogy, but its translation states that the English text is machine-translated and not yet fully proofread. Check the German edition and the underlying Greifswald/Stettin records for critical details.

The Georg–Christina–Hedwig–Christian connection is well supported by the family-history source. It is a **different route** into the family from the Borntin/Muttrin route leading to Cartz Ulrik.

## Note: Tychow / Reimer and Bartes: unresolved father conflict

Two sources conflict:
- The 2025 Tychow-Dubberow genealogy says Hans of Tychow I.24 had sons Reimer II.2 and Bartes II.3.
- L. Quandt's transcription of the 13 April 1477 collective fief letter in *Allgemeine Geschichte* names “Pribbeslaw zu Muttrin mit seinen Söhnen, die Brüder Reymer und Bartes zu [Groß-]Tichow”.
- The same general-history discussion says the earlier Tychow/Dubberow branch ancestor is not known from the older family records.

Therefore the father of Reimer and Bartes is disputed. Do not state Hans I.24 as proven, and do not merge the Muttrin and Tychow reconstructions until the original charter/authenticated copy and the later genealogy's reasoning are checked.

The branch below Bartes is explicitly stated in the 2025 Tychow-Dubberow biographies as:
**Bartes II.3 → Andreas (Drews) II.11 → Carsten II.21 → Bernd II.39 → Christian II.60.**
Reimer II.2's son is Bartez II.6; Bartez II.6 is not Andreas's father.

## Note: High-value corrections from Harbou (1897)

Harbou's article is especially useful because it explicitly warns that earlier Danish Kleist pedigrees by Benzon, Lengnick and others were often incomplete and inaccurate. It should be preferred over unattributed online pedigrees, while still being checked against the Danish records that Harbou cites.

1. **Christian Adam III.466 is the son-in-law route, not a son of Christian Casimir.** Harbou identifies Christian Adam as the husband of Barbara Juliane von Kleist and names Cartz Ulrik von Kleist (III.369) as her husband. This supports the existing tree route Christian Casimir II.82 → Barbara Juliane → Cartz Ulrik → Christian Adam III.466. Harbou's note 28 specifically says the *Dansk Biografisk Lexikon* had incorrectly made Christian Adam a son of his maternal grandfather Christian Casimir; the family history calls him Christian Adrian. This is an explicit warning against importing that older error.
2. **The supplied +4/+5 Danish descent does not match Harbou's account of Christian Adam's children.** Harbou lists Christian Adam's son Christian Frederik III.601, born 25 August 1743 and died 12 July 1799 at Bredsted, married Anna Margrethe Schubart. He does not list a son Frederik/Friedrich Carl Emil born 1742/43 and dying 1815. Nor does this Harbou branch support the supplied Christian Frederik Vilhelm (1774–1848), Sophie Frederikke (1776–1851) and Julie von Kleist (1814–1894) as descendants of Christian Adam. These names may belong to another Kleist line or reflect a conflation; do not attach them to p868 until their parents and source chain are established.
3. **The 1813–1822 Copenhagen branch data in the supplied text appears to be misattributed.** Harbou places Margrethe Christine Elisabeth, Frederik Christian III.853, Holgerine Henriette Caroline, Christian Conrad Sophus, Aadamine Frederikke Christiane, Riise Sophie Emilie and Johan Eduard Valentin among the children of Valentin Ulrik III.726 and Anna Matthea Bierg — not as children or grandchildren of the alleged Christian Frederik Vilhelm line. Harbou gives:
   - Margrethe Christine Elisabeth: born 25 August 1813 (not 25 March), died 9 March 1895.
   - Frederik Christian III.853: born 8 October 1815, died 26 November 1892 in Copenhagen.
   - Holgerine Henriette Caroline: born 17 April 1817, died 19 October 1845; married Hans Lindholm on **13 October 1838** (not 18 October).
   - Christian Conrad Sophus: born 8 February 1820, died **28 August 1880** (not unknown).
   - Aadamine Frederikke Christiane: born 2 August 1822, died 22 December 1874.
   - Riise Sophie Emilie: born 7 February 1824, died 7 February 1864.
   - Johan Eduard Valentin: born 2 December 1825.
   The supplied list omits two of these children and shifts the branch's parentage. Harbou's source references include portraits and photographs at the Royal Danish Library; these may help identify the relevant individuals.
4. **Herath/Herrath Juliane:** Harbou's text says she was a widow of Joachim Heinrich von Heydebreck at her father's death, was engaged in 1682 to a Baron von Borch, and later married a von Manteuffel. The second marriage is consistent with the supplied outline, but the proposed burial in Rendsburg on 31 October 1802 (or 1702) is not confirmed by the passage and needs a church-register check.
5. **Cartz spelling:** Harbou's note 25 explains that Cartz in Danish records became Carl, Curt or even Lauritz, and suggests it is a shortened form of Carsten. Preserve these as variants rather than creating separate people.

## Note: Harbou's Christian II.60 (d.1679) and the Tychow sequence

Harbou's 1897 passage (printed p.97 / PDF p.2) says Brandenburg colonel Christian von Kleist II.60 of Groß-Tychow inherited one part of the estate from his father and another part with his first wife Hedwig Maria von Kleist, daughter of Georg of Groß-Tychow and Christina von Woyten. His second wife was Barbara Eleonore von Kalckstein; he died in 1679. Harbou does not name Christian II.60's father in that paragraph. He calls Swedish cavalry captain Joachim Henning von Kleist II.59 his *Farbrodersøn* (paternal cousin), not half-brother.

The 2025 Tychow-Dubberow biographies instead explicitly give the chain:
**Bartes II.3 → Andreas (Drews) II.11 → Carsten II.21 → Bernd/Bernhard II.39 → Christian II.60 → Christian Casimir II.82 → Barbara Juliane → marriage to Cartz Ulrik III.369.**

The same 2025 Tychow volume says Reimer II.2's son was Bartez II.6, while Bartes II.3's son was Andreas II.11. Bartez II.6 is collateral, not Andreas's father.

However, the father of Reimer II.2 and Bartes II.3 remains disputed:
- 2025 Tychow-Dubberow genealogy: Hans I.24.
- L. Quandt's transcription of the 13 April 1477 collective fief letter: “Pribbeslaw zu Muttrin mit seinen Söhnen, die Brüder Reymer und Bartes zu [Groß-]Tichow”.
- The same general-history discussion says the earlier Tychow/Dubberow branch ancestor is not known from older family records.

Do not state Hans I.24 or Pribbeslaw as settled father until the charter/authenticated copy and the later reconstruction are reconciled. Also do not conflate Christian II.60 (Tychow numbering) with other von Kleist people bearing similar names or numbers in the Muttrin/Harbou numbering systems.

## Note: Independent re-check against the 2025 Tychow-Dubberow volume

The 2025 second edition of the Tychow-Dubberow biographies makes the Hans attribution explicit: its contents identify “the sons of Hans of Tichow (I.24)” as Reimer and Bartes; the biography says they were first cousins of Jürgen and Peter of Dubberow because their fathers were brothers. It then gives Reimer's wife as a von Born of Grassee and his sons as Bartez, Tessen and Joachim; Bartes's wife as a von Briesen and his children as Martin, Jacob, Andreas and a daughter who married Volze of Raddatz. This provides a more detailed published reconstruction of the Tychow branch and supports the later Hans tradition as the current family-association genealogy. Source: 2025 *Tychow-Dubberower Linie*, pp. 134–136, https://www.v-kleist.com/FG/Dubberow.pdf.

However, the same volume's introduction says that the name Hans in the generation after the earliest period rests on later genealogical material. Its 1477 reference does not quote the father phrase; it cites the charter for Reimer and Bartes being brothers. The actual 1477 text in Urk. 125, as printed by Quandt, separately says “Pribbeslaw of Muttrin with his sons, Reymer and Bartes, brothers at Tychow.” Thus the 2025 volume's Hans genealogy is a published modern synthesis, not independent primary confirmation of Hans's paternity. It appears to preserve the later family-genealogy reading while the 1477 text as transcribed names Pribbeslaw. This reinforces the decision not to change the canonical parentage or present the issue as settled.

The 2025 volume also states that Tessen of Dubberow was a nephew of Hans of Tichow, while his father was allegedly Peter of Dubberow; it explicitly says the family records only state the nephew relationship and identify his marriage and two sons. This helps define the collateral relationship but does not resolve whether Hans or Pribbeslaw fathered Reimer and Bartes.

## Note: 13. Harbou / von Kleist reconciliation cautions

1. `p3473` “Esther von Kameke”: one proposed reconstruction names an unnamed N.N. von Kameke as wife of Hans Jürgen von Kleist, while another source says Anna Esther von Kameke was wife of Pribislaff III.281. 3. `p6949` Hedwig Maria von Kleist: Harbou says she brought part of Groß Tychow into her marriage; if both spouses were von Kleist from different branches, record the cousin-marriage relationship accurately.
