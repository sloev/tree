## Foreløbigt migrationsspor

Otto Fr. Arends' *Stamtavle over Slægten Fischer fra Bryrup* (1934), printed p.23, reports that **Friderik Ernst Fristedt Fischer**, born 20 October 1846 and baptised 24 November 1846 at Fuur, left his studies at Odense Latinskole, went as a young man to the Cape Colony and worked in the diamond mines at Kimberley. Arends says he *probably* died there and was *probably* unmarried. Those two conclusions are explicitly uncertain in the source and must not be recorded as established facts.

The same book establishes a collateral descent from Major-General Salomon Gedde: Caroline Frideriche Wilhelmine Gedde, daughter of Salomon Gedde and Catharina Elisabeth Qvist, married Jens Henrich Fischer in 1796. Their Fischer descendants include Ingertha Cathrine Jensine Fischer, who married Carl Vilhelm Ferdinand Arends; their son Otto Frederik Arends wrote the 1934 genealogy. Arends' introduction warns that information on relatives abroad was incomplete and sometimes second-hand.

### Research implications

- This adds a specific South African destination/workplace to the wider Gedde-descended family's migration history.
- It is suitable for a migration map marker at Kimberley, labelled **“reported destination/workplace; route and death unverified.”**
- It does not establish that Fischer is in the user's direct descent line, nor that he died or was buried in Kimberley.
- Do not invent a departure point/date or draw a travel route without a passenger, emigration or passport record.
