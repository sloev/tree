Udvandrerprotokollen: 'Holgersen, Iver Peder, arbejder, født Tved, sidste bopæl Skalbjerg, 22 år, rejsemål Fresno, USA', 8. sep. 1910, med damperen United States. Polk's directories list an Iver Holgersen (Caroline) in the Alameda area in 1930, 1937 and 1939, consistently as a sheet-metal worker. A 1937 entry gives the initial P. A SAMPUBCO index also lists an Iver Peter Holgersen in a Lassen County Declaration of Intentions volume (vol. 2, p. 78; 1915–1919). These may be the same emigrant; the naturalization image must be checked. The 1930 directory also lists Jennie at Iver's address and Violet as a Berkeley student; neither is proven to be his child. A scholarly history identifies Violet D. Holgersen as a Berkeley student who taught nature study in Pasadena around 1930, citing a 5 November 1930 letter to Joseph Grinnell. This is a promising possible family connection, not a confirmed parent-child relationship.
