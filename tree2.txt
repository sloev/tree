Notes: 'Kongen af Langø'. He bought the island of Langø in Lindelse Nor in 1894
       with his wife Ane Marie, and in 1911 he built, by hand with help from
       sons and sons-in-law, the 334 m long causeway that made the island
       landfast. According to the family, Langø later passed to descendants
       named Pedersen. Several of the family's relatives from South Langeland
       emigrated to America. Granddaughter Lisbeth Skov, née Christensen,
       relates in 'En Langø-piges erindringer' that before 1894 the island had
       belonged to Hjortholm estate and later to three farms in Haugbølle,
       which used it for grazing: cattle were swum over in spring and home in
       autumn. A shoemaker had bought the island and built a simple house, but
       it went to forced auction, after which Marius and Ane Marie bought it.
       From granddaughter Lisbeth Skov's memoirs: Marius was in farming and a
       labourer, helped build Rudkøbing harbour and then helped dig the
       Copenhagen Free Port (he found a cannonball in the harbour, still on
       Langø). Back in Rudkøbing the couple bought a small smallholding on
       Spodsbjergvej, and Marius worked at Langelands Korn for 2 kroner a day.
       The owner of Baygården, chairman of Langelands Bank, who had taken over
       Langø at forced auction, persuaded them to buy the island for 4,000
       kroner with 1,600 kroner down. In 1894 the family moved out there with
       four children. From 11 Jan 1911 Marius and the sons built a causeway to
       Kædeby Haver, eventually 334 metres long and 6–8 metres wide; the
       authorities did not think he would finish it in his, his children's or
       grandchildren's time.

  - Cartz Ulrik von Gedde (III.369) — Danish officer, went into Danish service

    newer von Kleist register as the father of Cartz Ulrik.
  - Pribislaff (d. 1640) — earlier Borntin line.
  - Prebislaw til Borntin — mentioned 1621.
  - Hans Jürgen — landråd (councillor), son of Prebislaw.
  - Prebislaw til Muttrin, Borntin og Drenow — son of Hans Jürgen.
  - Prissebur / Klest de Densin — medieval mentions: 1248 Conradus and
    Bertoldus Clest in a Pomeranian document; 1289 Klest (Klest de Densin)

    ancestor and three sons.

   the underlying fief letters. The medieval persons should therefore not yet be
   entered as direct ancestors in the tree."
