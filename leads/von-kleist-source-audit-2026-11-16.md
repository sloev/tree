# von Kleist source audit — 16 November 2026

## Scope

This audit covers the 22 proposed people exp020–exp041 in the expanded research JSON and their proposed connection to existing people p3472 (Pribislaff von Kleist) and p3473 (Esther von Kameke). It also checks a related Tychow-Dubberow branch because the two reconstructions may be connected through the 1477 fief letter.

This is a source audit, not an import decision. The original uploaded exports are preserved unchanged. No canonical tree relationship is changed by this note.

The 2025 Muttrin volume explicitly explains that its early Stammtafel follows L. Quandt's 1871 arrangement, based on some contemporary documents but also much later family records. It calls the early arrangement a wahrscheinliche Anordnung (probable reconstruction). The volume also says that the old family-history texts have been reworked and that additions from archival records and other sources are marked separately. These qualifications must accompany the pedigree; the 2025 date of the edition does not make every early-generation claim a contemporary record.

## 1. Early Muttrin ancestry: what is actually supported

The Muttrin volume, printed pages 5–6 (accessible PDF text around lines 293–333), makes these distinctions:

- It reports that Prißebur, called “der Klest von Muttrin,” appears in Urkunde 83 of 1364 as a co-owner of land at Glineke.
- It says the names Jarßlaff and Henning as Prissebur's sons come from a family genealogy dated 1576 and an older letter, not from the 1364 wording alone.
- It identifies Henning M.8 as a son of Henning M.3, alive in 1435, and says his marriage to a von Wedelstett and seven sons are reported according to the old family genealogy.
- It identifies Bisprow III.1 as a son of Henning M.8 and refers to the 13 April 1477 fief letter; the biography then names his three sons Henning, Peter and Hans.

The 1364 occurrence and the reconstructed parent-child sequence are therefore different evidence propositions. The first is a dated documentary reference as reported by the 2025 edition; the second is a pedigree reconstruction using later family material.

### Existing person p3472 and p3473

The expanded JSON updates the existing person p3472 with a note identifying him as Pribislaff of Muttrin, Döbel and Borntin, died 1698, and identifies his wife as Anna Esther von Kameke. The family association's indexes corroborate the old family-history numbering and spouse-name form:

- NamenabfrageL lists Pribislaff auf Muttrin, III.281, died 1698, wife Anna Esther von Kameke.
- The spouse index lists Anna Esther von Kameke as wife of Pribislaff III.281.
- The daughters index lists children under Pribislaff III.281.

These indexes support identity reconciliation between existing p3472 and the proposed III.281 person, and between existing p3473 (previously “Esther von Kameke”) and Anna Esther. They are derivative indexes to the family history, not independent proof. The expanded JSON gives p3473 parents exp036 and exp037, but the canonical data still has no parents for p3472 or p3473.

**Important structural inconsistency:** exp033's note says his son Pribislaff III.281 is existing p3472, but the expanded JSON leaves p3472.father null and does not set a father field on exp033 linking him to p3472. This must be reconciled from the actual numbered biography before the proposed father-child link is encoded in either JSON or GEDCOM.

## 3. A material conflict in the early Tychow ancestry

The earlier review of the expanded Tychow text was too definite when it presented Hans I.24 as the established father of Reimer II.2 and Bartes II.3. The family-history sources contain a real conflict that must be preserved.

### What the 1477 charter is transcribed as saying

L. Quandt's Allgemeine Geschichte, in its discussion/transcription of the 13 April 1477 collective fief letter, lists “Pribbeslaw zu Muttrin mit seinen Söhnen, die Brüder Reymer und Bartes zu [Groß-]Tichow”. The accessible text is around line 1042 of:
https://www.v-kleist.com/FG/Genealogie/AllgemeineGeschichte.pdf

Read literally, this identifies Reimer and Bartes as sons of Pribbeslaw of Muttrin. The original charter or its authenticated copy should be checked before treating the transcription as definitive.

### What the 2025 Tychow-Dubberow biography says

The 2025 Tychow-Dubberow volume introduces Reimer and Bartes as the two sons of Hans of Tychow (I.24), then gives Reimer's son Bartez II.6 and Bartes's sons Martin II.9, Jacob II.10 and Andreas II.11:
https://www.v-kleist.com/FG/Dubberow.pdf, accessible lines 5616–5647 and 6021–6055.

This is a later family-history reconstruction. It conflicts with the literal wording of the 1477 charter as transcribed in Allgemeine Geschichte.

### What the general family history says about the uncertainty

In its reproduction/discussion of the old family genealogy, Allgemeine Geschichte says that the name of the Tychow/Dubberow branch's earlier brother/ancestor is not known from the family records (accessible lines 1373 and footnote 169 around line 1390). It also describes how later family genealogies assigned the three branches to named ancestors. This is another reason not to silently treat Hans I.24 as a contemporary-record fact.

### What remains secure within the Tychow line

The 2025 Tychow-Dubberow volume explicitly distinguishes the two sons/branches:

- Reimer II.2's son is Bartez II.6.
- Bartes II.3's sons include Andreas (Drews) II.11.
- Andreas II.11 is father of Carsten II.21.
- Carsten II.21 is father of Bernd II.39.
- Bernd II.39 is father of Christian II.60.

Thus the expanded text's chain Reimer II.2 → Bartez II.6 → Andreas II.11 is still wrong: Bartez II.6 is not Andreas's father. But the upstream father of Reimer and Bartes must be recorded as disputed between the 1477 charter transcription (Pribbeslaw of Muttrin) and the later Tychow genealogy (Hans I.24), pending examination of the underlying documents and the genealogy's reasoning.

This matters to the broader project because the Muttrin and Tychow lines may be connected through this disputed 1477 parentage. Do not merge their reconstructions or treat the proposed exp020–exp041 Muttrin chain as automatically proving the Tychow branch's parentage.
