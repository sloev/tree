## Gaardsted — archive-series correction

The online alphabetical extract for Rolsøgård gods G302 identifies the relevant 1787 entry as:

- Niels Jensen Degn in Vrinners, probate no. 11, 14 May 1787, folio 39.
- The transcription names wife Maren Lauridsdatter, children and stepchildren, then reads: “Arv i boet til afdødes stedbørn efter Christen Christensen Tohde? foged i Dejret (skifte 1764) til datter Karen Christensdatter LV: broder Claus Gaardsted forpagter på Vosnæsgård.”

Source: https://slaegt.dk/media/jnnnvuml/alfabetisk-navneliste-og-skifteuddrag-til-rols%C3%B8g%C3%A5rd-gods-g302-mols-herred-1778-1816-1818.pdf

The extract is strong evidence that the 1787 estate mentions Karen Christensdatter as a stepchild/heir associated with an earlier probate for a bailiff named Christen Christensen “Tohde?” in Dejret, and that Claus Gaardsted is described as her brother and legal guardian, tenant at Vosnæsgård. It does **not** by itself resolve the uncertain surname reading or establish that Christen was Claus's father. The original 1787 folio still needs to be read.

A significant archive-series point: the G302 index is explicitly for Rolsøgård probate material **1778–1816 (1818)**. The cited 1764 probate therefore cannot simply be assumed to be in this same indexed series. Rigsarkivet's guidance says that before 1793 probate jurisdiction for non-freeholding rural residents depended on the estate/tenure: estate owners handled the probate of their tenants and employees; county-level probate series handled rural non-tenants. The correct 1764 archive creator must be identified from Christen's tenure/employer and the contemporary estate boundaries, rather than guessing “Rolsøgård 1764.”

Rigsarkivet guidance: https://www.rigsarkivet.dk/vejledning/skifter-og-testamenter/skiftearkivalier-kom-godt-i-gang/

## Von Kleist / Muttrin — additional source comparison

A second, more important source pass checked the 2025 Muttrin volume against the family association's 2021/2022 *Urkundenbuch* edition.

### 1364: Prissebur of Muttrin is a real documentary reference

The 2025 Muttrin volume, printed pages 5–6, explicitly says that the second son of Klest (M.1) is mentioned in Urkunde 83 of 1364 as a co-owner of land at Glineke: “Prißebur, der Klest heißt von Muttrin.” It then says the names Jarßlaff and Henning as Prissebur's sons come from an early family genealogy of 1576 and an older letter in which Prissebur acted for himself and his two sons concerning Glineke.

This is not the same as the acquisition of Damen around 1365. The earlier correction in Segment 1 of the extension that said there was no 1364 reference was wrong as a blanket statement. The 1364 reference is present in the Muttrin volume; the c.1365 date concerns Damen.

The 2025 authors expressly qualify the early pedigree: Quandt's arrangement uses individual contemporary documents together with family records made considerably later, and is a *wahrscheinliche Anordnung* (probable arrangement). Accordingly:
- Prissebur's appearance in 1364 is a documented clue reported by the modern family-history edition, with the original Urkunde 83 still to be inspected.
- The parent link from Klest to Prissebur and the sons Jarßlaff/Henning have a different evidentiary basis (later family genealogy / older letter as reported by the edition).
- Later generations should be checked against each cited document rather than assigned the same confidence as the 1364 name occurrence.

Source: https://www.v-kleist.com/FG/Muttrin/Muttrin.pdf, printed pp. 5–6, lines 293–320 in the accessible text.

### 1477: the collective enfeoffment gives an independent structural anchor

The 2021/2022 *Urkundenbuch*, Urkunde 125, dated Belgard 13 April 1477, reproduces the text of Duke Bugislaw X's collective fief and common-hand letter. The edition says it is based on a 16th-century authenticated copy in the Landesarchiv (Tit. IX. Sect. 88. Nr. I. fol. 1), with further transsumpts/retranssumpts listed in note 128.

The charter names:
- Bisprowe at Muttrin with sons Hennige, Petern and Hanse;
- Dubbeszlaff at Damen with sons Drewese, Curde, Bisprowe, Petern and Pribbetzlaue;
- Pribbeslaff at Muttrin with sons Reymer and Bartes;
- brothers at Tychow and Dubberow, and the Vilnow and Raddatz representatives.

The charter itself says the Kleists present testified that they descended from one “Older Vader” who had three sons, from whom came the Muttrin-Damen, Tychow-Dubberow, and Vilnow-Raddatz branches. The edition's explanatory footnotes assign genealogical numbers to some people, but those are editorial identifications and must be kept distinct from the medieval wording itself.

This is valuable evidence for the existence and structure of the three named branches by 1477. It does not independently prove every earlier generation back to 1364, and it does not by itself connect the 1477 men to Cartz Ulrik or the Danish Gedde branch.

Source: https://www.v-kleist.com/FG/Gesamt.pdf, Urkunde 125, printed pp. 73–74 / accessible lines 2760–2782.

### 1402–1439 bridge records reported by the 2025 Muttrin volume

The same 2025 introduction identifies further cited records:
- Urkunde 100, 2 February 1407: Kleists act as guarantors for a cousin; named men include Kurt Klest von Damen, Hennyngh Clest von Bruskow and Hennyngh Clest von Mutteryn.
- Urkunde 92, 1389: Pritzbor appears as a cleric at Zatkow.
- Urkunden 93–95, 1402: young Bispraw Klest appears among the regional vassals/arbitrators and as a witness.
- Urkunde 105, 18 August 1439: Hermen and Jurjen Klest, sons of Curt (M.4), are listed in an agreement.
- Urkunde 104, 1435: Henning (M.8) was still living.

These references are a practical generation-by-generation audit path. They are still secondary descriptions until the underlying charter texts and identities are checked.

### Numbering warning

The 1477 Urkundenbuch notes explain that genealogical numbering begins around this charter and that the people named there form the heads of the respective branches. The 2025 Muttrin volume also uses its own M. numbering in its early Stammtafel, while the extension worksheet uses III. numbers and Harbou's Danish article uses family-history numbers. Do not assume M.1, III.281, and a similarly named person in a user-supplied Geneanet reconstruction are interchangeable. The five-number sequence III.22 → III.54 → III.114 → III.186 → III.281 remains an audit target, not an accepted chain, until every numbered biography is located and matched.

### What is now usable in the tree's research narrative

1. Prißebur/Prissebur is reported in the 2025 Muttrin volume as named in a 1364 document as a Muttrin land co-owner.
2. The 1477 collective fief letter records three main Kleist branches and names their representatives and sons.
3. The early generations between those anchors are partly reconstructed from later family records and selected contemporary documents.
4. The Danish descent from Cartz Ulrik / Pribislaff is a separate later chain requiring its own source reconciliation.

## Christian von Kleist II.60 (d.1679) — Harbou checked against the new Z.ai lead

The relevant passage in H. W. Harbou, “Slægten von Kleist i Danmark,” *Personalhistorisk Tidsskrift* 1897, p. 97 in the printed article / page 2 in the PDF text, has now been reread directly.

Harbou states that the Brandenburg colonel Christian von Kleist (II.60) of Groß-Tychow:
- inherited one part of the estate from his father;
- received another part with his first wife Hedwig Maria von Kleist, daughter of Georg of Groß-Tychow and Christina von Woyten;
- married second Barbara Eleonore von Kalckstein;
- died in 1679, after which his four sons from the first marriage divided the Pomeranian estates on 1 June; there were also one daughter from the first marriage and one son and one daughter from the second.

Harbou does **not** name Christian II.60's father in this paragraph. He separately says that Swedish cavalry captain Joachim Henning von Kleist (II.59) of Groß-Tychow was Christian's *Farbrodersøn*. This Danish kinship term denotes a paternal uncle's son / cousin relationship; it does not mean half-brother. Harbou therefore does not establish the proposed sequence Christian II.60 ← Bernd II.39 ← Carsten II.21 ← Joachim II.22.

The Z.ai-supplied candidate sequence:
- Christian II.60 ← Bernd II.39 + Elisabeth von Manteuffel;
- Bernd II.39 ← Carsten II.21 “the Elder” + Hedwig von Heydebreck;
- Carsten II.21 ← Joachim II.22;
- further ancestry to Bartez the Younger and Reimer II.2;

is retained as a **new unverified lead**, not a finding. It was not located in Harbou's 1897 Danish article in this pass (searches for “Bernd (II.39)” and “Joachim (II.22)” return no matching text). The next required action is to open the exact numbered biographies II.60, II.39, II.21 and II.22 in the relevant German *Geschichte des Geschlechts von Kleist* / family-history volume, inspect the original printed page images and references, then trace each parent link to its cited contemporary record. The family-history numbering is not interchangeable with the III. numbering used in Harbou's Muttrin section.

## Tychow-Dubberow chain: direct check of the 2025 numbered biographies

Source inspected: *History of the Family v. Kleist, Third Part, Second Department: Tychow-Dubberower line*, 2nd revised edition, German 2025; English machine translation 2026:

### The key correction to the Z.ai chain

The numbered biographies do **not** give the sequence Reimer II.2 → Bartez II.6 → Andreas II.11. They show two brothers and two separate son-lines:

- Hans of Tychow (I.24) had sons Reimer II.2 and Bartes II.3 (printed pp. 121–122 / PDF pp. 121–122).
- Reimer II.2 (born c.1425) and his wife, a von Born of the Grassee house, had three sons: Bartez II.6, Tessen II.7 and Joachim II.8. Bartez II.6 died c.1527 and married a von Zozenow; their six sons are listed as II.15–20 (printed pp. 122–123 / PDF pp. 122–123).
- Bartes II.3 was Reimer's brother. Bartes and his wife from the von Briesen family had three sons: Martin II.9, Jacob II.10 and Andreas (Drews) II.11 (printed p.122 / PDF pp.122–123).
- Andreas II.11 († after 1546) was the father of Carsten II.21 and Joachim II.22 (printed pp.132–133 / PDF pp.132–133).
- Carsten II.21 († before 1618), called “the Elder” to distinguish him from his cousin Carsten II.19, was married first to a von Hechthausen and around 1575 to his niece Hedwig von Heydebreck. The volume lists eight sons, including Bernhard II.39 (printed pp.133–134 / PDF pp.133–134).
- Bernd/Bernhard II.39 († after 1665), sixth son of Carsten II.21, was married to Elisabeth von Manteuffel of Pribbernow and Broitz. The biography says she bore him Christian II.60 and Andreas Joachim II.61 (printed pp.134–135 / PDF pp.134–135).
- Christian II.60 (†1679) is explicitly introduced as a son of Bernd II.39. The biography names his two wives: first Hedwig Maria von Kleist, daughter of Georg II.55; second Barbara Eleonora von Kalkstein. It lists four sons and one daughter from the first marriage and one son and one daughter from the second (printed pp.142–145 / PDF pp.143–145).
- Christian Casimir II.82 (born 1654, died 19 Feb 1722) is explicitly described as Christian II.60's second son. His wife was Anna Hedwig von Fürst; their daughter Barbara Juliane married Lieutenant Colonel Cartz Ulrich von Kleist (III.369) (printed pp.145–146 / PDF pp.146–147).

Thus the supported paternal branch is:
Hans I.24 → Bartes II.3 → Andreas (Drews) II.11 → Carsten II.21 → Bernd/Bernhard II.39 → Christian II.60 → Christian Casimir II.82 → Barbara Juliane → marriage to Cartz Ulrich III.369.

Reimer II.2 is the brother of Bartes II.3, not the direct father of Andreas II.11. Bartez II.6 is Reimer's son and belongs to a collateral branch. The two lines share the earlier Hans I.24 ancestor, but Bartez II.6 must not be inserted between Reimer II.2 and Andreas II.11.

### Evidence character and cautions

This is a meaningful advance: the revised family-history volume explicitly states each parent-child relationship in the numbered biographies and cites older feudal letters, investitures, tax registers, family records and other archive materials. It is still a modern secondary genealogical compilation, not a substitute for independently inspecting each cited original record. The volume itself distinguishes documentary support from family-table claims in places; for example, it reports the marriage of Reimer II.2 to a von Born based on family records, and Carsten II.21's marriages according to von der Osten. Keep the source type visible per link.

The English PDF labels itself a machine translation made in 2026 and says it has not yet been proofread. The German original should be checked for exact terms, especially “Nichte” and “Farbrodersohn,” and for any translation ambiguity.

## Correction — Tychow/Reimer/Bartes father is disputed

A further source comparison requires qualification of the preceding Tychow-Dubberow section. The 2025 Tychow-Dubberow volume states that Reimer II.2 and Bartes II.3 were sons of Hans of Tychow I.24. However, L. Quandt's *Allgemeine Geschichte* transcribes the 13 April 1477 collective fief letter as naming “Pribbeslaw zu Muttrin mit seinen Söhnen, die Brüder Reymer und Bartes zu [Groß-]Tichow” (accessible text around line 1042). The same general-history discussion says that the name of the earlier Tychow/Dubberow branch ancestor is not known from the older family records (around lines 1373 and 1390).

These statements conflict. The 1477 transcription appears to name Pribbeslaw of Muttrin as father; the later Tychow genealogy assigns the brothers to Hans I.24. Until the original charter/authenticated copy and the genealogical reasoning are checked, the father of Reimer and Bartes must remain unresolved. The descent from Bartes II.3 to Andreas II.11, Carsten II.21, Bernd II.39 and Christian II.60 remains explicitly stated in the 2025 Tychow-Dubberow biographies. Bartez II.6 is Reimer II.2's son and is not the father of Andreas II.11.

This also means the earlier statement that the whole chain begins with Hans I.24 was too definite. The corrected source audit is in leads/von-kleist-source-audit-2026-11-16.md.
