THE GEOGRAPHIC CONTEXT — A KEY NEW INSIGHT
==============================================================================

The Egernsund / Nybøl / Snogbæk / Paakjær geography is critical:

Egernsund (Egernsund sogn, Nybøl herred, Sønderborg amt)
  — Where Else Gedde Jensen lived with Peter Frederiksen from 1931
  — Where Lorenz was born 12 Jan 1932
  — Where the civil register entry was made (bsid 37658)

Nybøl sogn (Nybøl herred, Sønderborg amt)
  — Contains Paakjær gård (Sophus Petersen's family farm)
  — Contains the village of Nybøl where Detlef Krogh sr. settled 1883
  — Where Vilhelm Krogh's siblings Detlef, Anna, and Christine were born

Sottrup sogn (Nybøl herred, Sønderborg amt)
  — Contains Vester Snogbæk where Vilhelm Krogh worked as karl in 1932

KEY DISTANCES (approximate):
  Egernsund — Nybøl (Paakjær):  ~4 km
  Egernsund — V. Snogbæk (Sottrup): ~5 km
  Nybøl — V. Snogbæk:  ~3 km

ALL THREE PLACES ARE WITHIN WALKING DISTANCE.
The Krogh family and the Petersen family of Paakjær lived in the same
immediate rural area. Sophus Petersen and Vilhelm Krogh likely knew each
other personally — they were contemporaries, both unmarried in 1932,
both from the same Nybøl/Sottrup community.

This geographic proximity is what makes the family's account plausible:
if Sophus could not acknowledge paternity (as a gårdejer-søn and heir to
the family farm), he may have asked Vilhelm — a local landarbejder
without prospects — to take the blame. Vilhelm, who was 23 at the time
and a karl on someone else's farm, had less to lose.

THE PATERNITY DECLARATION PROCESS — WHAT WE KNOW
==============================================================================

The Egernsund civil birth register (Standesamt Egernsund) recorded:

1. BIRTH ANMELDELSE (16 January 1932):
   Else Gedde Frederiksen, f. Jensen, bore a son on 12 January 1932 at
   5:30 AM in Egernsund. The birth was reported (anmeldt) on 16 January
   1932 — 4 days after birth, which was the standard deadline.
   Source: AO:Egernsund standsregister, fødte 1928-33, 1932 nr. 4
   (bsid 37658, billede 125).
   Note: At this point, no father was registered. The child was
   initially "illegitimate" (uegte).

2. PATERNITY DECLARATION BY WILHELM KROGH (5 April 1932):
   Wilhelm Krogh, landarbejder from Snogbæk, declared that he was the
   father of the child. This was done before the local authority
   (probably the sognefoged or Standesamt Egernsund).
   Source: cited in the marginal note (see #4).

3. COURT DECISION (10 June 1932):
   A court decision (retsafgørelse) was rendered on 10 June 1932.
   This suggests the paternity was not uncontested — possibly:
   (a) Else or her family contested Wilhelm's claim, or
   (b) The authority required judicial confirmation before registering.
   The court confirmed Wilhelm's paternity declaration.
   Source: cited in the marginal note.
   Likely archive location:
   • Aabenraa-Sønderborg Amt, Alimentationsjournal 1932 (1920-1935 file
     series, requires unprinted finding aid at Landsarkivet for
     Sønderjylland reading room, Aabenraa).
   • Alternatively, civil court records at the same archive.

4. MARGINAL NOTE IN CIVIL REGISTER (11 July 1932):
   The registrar added a marginal note to the 1932 nr. 4 entry, dated
   11 July 1932, recording Wilhelm Krogh as the legal father based on
   the 5 April declaration and 10 June court decision.
   Source: AO:Egernsund standsregister, fødte 1928-33, 1932 nr. 4
   (bsid 37658, billede 125) — marginal note visible on the image.

5. BAPTISM (1 January 1933, Broager Vestre distrikt):
   Lorenz was baptized in Broager Vestre distrikt as "Lorenz Heinrich"
   with Peter Frederiksen (Else's husband) recorded as the father in the
   church book. Faddere (godparents):
   • Peter Frederiksen (stepfather, Else's husband)
   • Arbejdsmand Lorenz Frederiksen + hustru Catharina (Egernsund) —
     Peter's parents
   • Gårdejer Jens Jensens hustru fra Dalsgaard — Else's maternal aunt
     (Christine, Paula's sister)
   Source: AO:Broager Vestre distrikt kontraministerialbog 1906-48,
   fødte 1933 nr. 1 (bsid 201141, billede 112).
   Note: This baptismal entry uses PETER FREDERIKSEN as the father,
   which matches the legal/social parenthood (Peter was married to Else
   by this time, having married 15 November 1931). The civil register
   randnote separately records WILHELM KROGH as the legal paternity-
   declared father.

This dual documentation — civil register (Wilhelm) vs. church book
(Peter) — is consistent with the family account that Peter Frederiksen
"was paid to marry Else" and accept the child as his own, while the
civil paternity was assigned to Wilhelm through his declaration.

  • This dual structure (social father = Peter, legal father = Wilhelm)
    was common in cases where the biological father could not or would
    not marry the mother.

WHY WOULD WILHELM KROGH DECLARE PATERNITY?
==============================================================================

Hypotheses (NOT verified — to be tested against the court record):

H1: Wilhelm was the actual biological father.
  • The civil process was straightforward: he acknowledged paternity,
    the court confirmed.
  • This is the simplest explanation and matches the legal record.
  • But it contradicts the family account, which is consistent across
    multiple independent family sources.

H2: Wilhelm was paid or coerced to declare paternity on behalf of Sophus.
  • Sophus, as gårdmandssøn from Paakjær, had property/inheritance
    prospects that would be complicated by an illegitimate child.
  • Wilhelm, as a landarbejder (karl) without property, had less to lose.
  • The two men were neighbors (Paakjær and Snogbæk are ~3 km apart)
    and likely knew each other.
  • Payment or other consideration could have been arranged.
  • This matches the family account.

H3: Wilhelm was forced to declare paternity after losing a court case.
  • A "retsafgørelse" can be the result of either uncontested
    acknowledgment or contested litigation. Without reading the actual
    case file, we cannot distinguish.
  • Under Danish paternity law (which applied to Aabenraa-Sønderborg
    amt from 1 April 1932), if a man was accused of paternity and could
    not disprove it, he could be ordered to pay child support
    (alimentation) — even if he denied biological paternity.
  • Some men accepted paternity declarations to avoid prolonged
    litigation.

H4: Wilhelm was innocent but accepted the declaration out of friendship
    or family obligation.
  • He may have been Sophus's friend or relative.
  • No blood relationship between the Krogh and Petersen families has
    been documented, but the geographic proximity (Nybøl sogn) suggests
    social ties.

Distinguishing these hypotheses requires:
  • Reading the actual 1932 court case file at Landsarkivet for
    Sønderjylland, Aabenraa (Aabenraa-Sønderborg Amt 1932
    alimentationsjournal).
  • DNA testing of Lorenz's descendants against:
    (a) descendants of Wilhelm Krogh's siblings (Detlef, Peter, Anna,
        Christine Marie — note Christine Marie died age 18 with no
        descendants).
    (b) descendants of Sophus Petersen's siblings (Lorenz Peter, Peter,
        Jørgen, Marie, Hans, Viggo) and of Sophus's legitimate
        daughters (Gerda and her sisters).

THE DANISH PATERNITY LAW OF 1932 — LEGAL CONTEXT
==============================================================================

In 1932, Aabenraa-Sønderborg Amt applied Danish paternity law (since
the area had been returned to Denmark in 1920 after the plebiscite).

Key legal principles:
  • An unmarried mother could name the alleged father.
  • The alleged father could acknowledge paternity voluntarily
    (faderskabserklæring) — this is what Wilhelm did on 5 April 1932.
  • If the alleged father denied paternity, the case went to civil court
    (retssag), where evidence (witnesses, correspondence) was weighed.
  • If the court found paternity likely, it issued a "retsafgørelse"
    ordering the man to pay child support (alimentation).
  • Paternity could be assigned even without biological certainty if
    the evidence was strong enough.

The 10 June 1932 court decision in Lorenz's case could be either:
  • Uncontested confirmation of Wilhelm's voluntary declaration, OR
  • Contested litigation that Wilhelm lost.

Without reading the actual case file, we cannot distinguish. The case
file is at:
  Landsarkivet for Sønderjylland, Aabenraa
  Archive: Aabenraa Amt 1920-1932 / Aabenraa-Sønderborg Amt 1932-1972
  Series: Alimentationsjournal 1932 (or Almindelig Journal 1932, or
          Journalsager / Alimentationssager)
  Access: 75-year closure period has expired (1932 + 75 = 2007).
  Finding aid: Unprinted, in reading room at Aabenraa.

To request access:
  Contact: Landsarkivet for Sønderjylland, Aabenraa
  Website: https://www.rigsarkivet.dk/finding-aids/guides/landsarkivet-for-soenderjylland/
  Guide: https://www.rigsarkivet.dk/vejledning/faderskab-og-adoption/faderskabssager-kom-godt-i-gang/
