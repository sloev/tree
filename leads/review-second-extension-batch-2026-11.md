## Overall assessment

This is a substantial and useful research plan. It distinguishes document schedules from ready-to-enter people, gives many exact parish/archive targets, and consolidates 49 open items (O-1–O-49). The main risk is that the patch and some confidence labels promote inferences beyond what the cited derivative extracts establish. Treat the patch as a proposal, not an executable import.

## High-priority cautions

### 1. Gaardsted / Rolsøgård — do not apply the structural patch as written

The patch's Section A creates Christen Christensen Tohde as the definite father of both Karen Christensdatter and existing person Claus Gaardsted; it assigns Claus the name `Claus Christensen` and adds FAMC/FAM136.

That exceeds the evidence currently transcribed. The 1787 G302 index extract is an entry in Niels Jensen Degn's probate, not Karen's probate. Its wording appears to mention inheritance to the deceased's stepchildren after Christen Christensen Tohde (question-marked/uncertain reading), a daughter Karen Christensdatter, and her brother Claus Gaardsted acting as lavværge. This is a strong sibling/kinship lead, but the syntax does not securely establish that Tohde was the biological or legal father of both, nor that Claus used Christensen as a patronymic. The 1764 probate itself and the full 1787 probate folio are still required.

**Recommendation:** do not apply F136, the father-child links, or the name change to `p72`. Keep the sibling clue and possible Tohde connection in notes with explicit uncertainty until the original records are read. This is consistent with the earlier review in [review-new-extension-files-2026-11.md](review-new-extension-files-2026-11.md).

### 2. Ove Samuel Gedde — two-person hypothesis is plausible, not yet proved

Segment 12 proposes:
- Ove Samuel I, born 28 February 1778, died young; and
- Ove Samuel II, born 28 May 1788 in Fredrikstad, died 10 March 1843 in Helsingør.

The evidence it cites is the ordinal in a compiled genealogy, a separate modern genealogy profile, Harbou's 1778 birth attached to the man who married in 1806 and died in 1843, and a biographical argument from Hans Christopher Gedde's Norwegian postings. This is a coherent explanation of the 1778/1788 conflict, but the ordinal and compiled profiles are not independent proof of two people. The posting argument is circumstantial. A birth in 1788 would make the 1806 groom about 18; possible, but not decisive.

**Recommendation:** retain the two-person hypothesis as a prominent research lead. Do not create Ove Samuel I or change `p216` to 1788/Fredrikstad until the 1778 burial and 1788 Fredrikstad baptism have been checked. Segment 12's own O-46 and O-47 are the right next steps. Also inspect Harbou's full treatment before attributing the conflation to Harbou as a demonstrated error.

### 3. Fischer sisters — preserve the correction as a sourced proposal, not yet primary fact

The batch proposes Frederikke Christiane Fischer (1745–1787) as Hans Christopher Gedde's first wife and Øllegaard Sophie Fischer (1752–1821) as his second. The supplied support is a genealogy quoting a family account and a Wikipedia-derived reference in the patch. This may resolve the wife-order problem, but it is not equivalent to locating the marriages, burials, or a reliable contemporary family record.

**Recommendation:** retain both candidate identities and the proposed order in the research notes; verify against the full Harbou text and primary records before structural changes. Do not silently change `p433` from first to second wife as if primary-verified.

### 4. Lorenz's paternity and DNA plan — keep relationship categories separate

Segment 8 correctly wants all three paternal roles recorded, but its wording needs care:
- Sophus Petersen: family account identifies him as biological father.
- Wilhelm Krogh: the 1932 civil-register marginal note/court process records legal acknowledgement/registered paternity; this is not, by itself, genetic proof.
- Peter Frederiksen: social/stepfather and surname-giver, according to the family account.

Do not label Krogh “biological per the register” as a proven biological fact. A legal paternity ruling and a DNA conclusion are different kinds of evidence. Likewise, the claim that Peter was paid to marry Else should remain attributed to its source unless independently documented.

The proposed MyHeritage shared-match subtraction can help form candidate clusters, but the stated cM bands and the assertion that matches below 30 cM are “noise” are too categorical. Shared-match lists alone do not identify a father; relationship ranges overlap, and small matches are not automatically irrelevant. Use documented match amounts, relationship hypotheses, triangulation/segment information where available, known relatives on the Frederiksen side, and independent documentary evidence. Treat the proposed descendant test as useful, not as an automatic “closes it outright” result.

### 5. Vibygård / Syv patch — wait for the 1822 marriage

The 1820 Vibygaard probate extract may identify Maren Poulsdatter's adult children, including a gartner named Jens/Hans Jensen (as transcribed in the source files). The patch then attaches the four children to existing `a66` and adds the 1820 Maren marriage as his first marriage. The 1822 Syv marriage is still listed as open (O-11). The shared occupation and locality are a good identity clue, but the patch itself acknowledges that the marriage entry has not confirmed the match.

**Recommendation:** do not add F137/F138 or the four child links until the 1822 marriage and original probate wording establish the identity and relationships. Keep the named probate children as provisional individuals/leads, not confirmed children of `a66`.

### 6. Count and patch consistency

Segment 8 says there are 11 new persons in clear GEDCOM-ready form. Segment 10's “New persons — GEDCOM-ready” list actually enumerates seven individual IDs (Christen, Maren, four children, Willum), while Section A of the patch also creates Karen. The count/list/patch do not reconcile cleanly. The patch also contains proposed relationships that are not ready for import, as noted above.

**Recommendation:** reconcile the count and inventory by unique person ID before any import. Check every proposed ID against the existing data and the duplicate-ID issue already documented for `p13`.

## Useful new research routes to preserve

- **Assing / Jens Peder Larsen:** the leap-day 29 February 1812 clue is worth following, but the parents remain unverified until the Assing birth/baptism register is inspected.
- **Hoven mill household:** the fixed mill property, widow/owner census entries and aftægts household clues make this a practical archival search.
- **Nybøl / Paakjær:** the distinction between church registers and post-1867 German Standesamt records is important. Verify each generation through the appropriate register; the farm's 1879 building and fire-insurance records are promising collateral sources.
- **Malmö / Lovisa Larsson:** Malmö birth and household registers, then the 1885 migration records, are a sound route to identify her mother and trace Niels Larsen.
- **Fuglslev / Rousing:** the file itself correctly marks the upper generations as name-pattern/household hypotheses. The herred appointment, 1787/1801 censuses and parish records should be read before extending the line.
- **Niels Jørgensen:** resolve the ambiguous “Hove/Høje” wording from the full 1854 marriage entry before selecting a baptism.
- **Herfølge/Herlufmagle and Broager/Nybøl branches:** the proposed marriage and parish register sequence is actionable; preserve the distinction between documented fosterage and biological parentage.
- **Family-held Langeland documents:** scanning and preserving the one-copy dødsliste and memoir is a worthwhile source-preservation priority.

## Recommended order of work

1. Read the full Harbou text and reconcile each citation, especially the Gedde and von Kleist entries.
2. Obtain the original 1764 Tohde probate and full 1787 G302 folio before deciding Claus/Karen parentage.
3. Check Ove Samuel's 1778 burial and the 1788 Fredrikstad baptism.
4. Check the 1822 Syv marriage and full 1820 Vibygaard probate before linking Maren and the four children to `a66`.
5. Verify the Fischer marriages/deaths from contemporary records.
6. Continue the document schedules in Segments 7 and 9, recording unsuccessful searches as well as positive results.
7. Only then prepare a corrected patch, with each person and relationship separately sourced and typed (biological, legal, social/step, foster/guardian, household).
