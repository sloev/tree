## What the new batch contributes

### Segment 13 — Harbou reconciliation worksheet

The worksheet identifies 29 PHT:1897 citations in the GEDCOM and gives a practical row-by-row process:
- find Harbou's own numbering for each person;
- transcribe what the Danish and German versions actually say;
- classify each citation as CONFIRM, CORRECT, HARBOU ONLY, or SILENT;

This is a strong audit method. The special checks are well chosen: Esther von Kameke, Anna von Fürst/Fürsten, Hedvig Maria von Kleist, and Cartz Ulrik's military service. **Caution:** the worksheet is a blank audit instrument, not the reconciliation itself. Its existence does not verify any of the 29 citations. The instruction to read both language versions and record differences should be followed before changing citations or person data.

## Material disagreements and import cautions

### 1. Claus Gaardsted's father — A-grade claim remains unverified here

Segment 15 says one clause in a 1787 probate names Claus's father, office, parish and probate year, and calls the finding A-grade. But the underlying transcription previously supplied in the extension says, in substance, that inheritance in Niels Jensen Degn's estate concerned the deceased's stepchildren after Christen Christensen Tohde (surname itself marked with a question mark), including daughter Karen Christensdatter, with brother Claus Gaardsted acting as lavværge.

That wording strongly suggests a family connection and makes the 1764 probate an excellent target. It does not, without the original folio and careful reading of the full sentence, safely establish that Christen Christensen Tohde was Claus's father or that Claus's surname/patronymic should become Christensen. Segment 14 itself reinforces the uncertainty: the name Tohde has no independent online occurrence and the transcriber marked it uncertain.

**Decision:** preserve the new A-grade assertion as the extension author's conclusion, but do not apply the father-child relationship, `Claus Christensen` name change, or FAMC until the original 1787 folio and the 1764 probate have been examined. The new negatives log does not resolve the underlying syntax.

### 2. Vibygård probate — primary record does not alone establish identity with `a66`

Segment 15 says the 1820 probate has four adults, occupations and a husband, and claims four-way convergence on the identification with the existing Syv/Viby person. Segment 14 still says the 1822 Syv marriage was not found online and must be checked page-by-page. The earlier patch proposal itself treats the 1822 marriage as an outstanding check.

A probate may securely establish the named relationships within the deceased's own household. The further step—identifying the named husband/guardian with existing `a66`—is a separate identity resolution. “Four-way convergence” needs to be unpacked into the actual independent records and matching details before it is accepted.

**Decision:** preserve the four probate-named people and their reported relationships as a high-priority lead. Do not attach them to `a66` or create the proposed family structure until the probate original and the 1822 Syv marriage (or equally strong independent identity evidence) have been read.

### 3. Fischer sisters and Hans Christopher Gedde

Segment 15 calls Hans Christopher as Ove Samuel's father and the Fischer sisters B-grade, citing an encyclopaedia and a family account. This is useful secondary evidence and may justify a carefully attributed note. It is not equivalent to primary marriage/death evidence. Segment 13's Harbou audit may improve the picture, but the worksheet is not yet completed.

**Decision:** retain the proposed wife order and full dates as sourced claims/hypotheses; do not silently overwrite the live family structure until the source passages are inspected and the conflict with existing evidence is documented.

### 4. Ove Samuel split

Segment 15 says the conflation is well-evidenced but that the correct birth date is unresolved. That is a fair summary of the hypothesis, but the 1778 burial and 1788 Fredrikstad baptism remain open. Do not create a second Ove Samuel or change `p216` to 1788 until those records are checked.

### 5. von Kleist extension

Segment 15 appropriately advises against importing the five-generation extension wholesale. Segment 13 gives the right next step: reconcile Harbou's numbering, identify the gaps, and only then test the Pomeranian generations against the family-history volumes and Greifswald charters. Keep the distinction between the 1364 Muttrin document reference and the c.1365 Damen acquisition; the earlier C-1 correction should not erase the 1364 reference where it belongs.
