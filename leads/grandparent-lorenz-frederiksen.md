### Medieval-to-early-modern von Kleist evidence audit (16 November 2026)

This is a source-by-source evaluation of the medieval and early-modern leads preserved in the archived branch dossier, cross-branch ledger, expanded export audit and the current family-history publications. It does **not** establish a continuous descent from the medieval Klest to canonical Pribislaff III.281 (p3472). The following evidence grades describe what each item supports, not the prestige of the family or the authority of a later pedigree.

#### A-1. The earlier Conradus Clest and Bertoldus Clest (1248–1269)

The 1248–1269 evidence concerns a separate, earlier Kleist-named pair in the Oder/Stettin region. It must be kept distinct from the 1289 Belgard witness group until a connection is demonstrated.

- **Urk. 26 (1248):** Conradus Clest appears among witnesses to Barnim I's grant involving Tamzowa and Radekow. The Urkundenbuch cites the original in the Pommersches Provinzialarchiv, Schr. I, Chat. 1, Nr. 6. A later note says Conradus and Theodoricus Bawarus were still squires in 1248.
- **Urk. 27 (17 June 1249):** the Colbatz monastery matricula gives “Bertoldus et Conradus Clest fratres” (brothers). This is an explicit relationship in the documentary tradition, although this edition is from the monastery's register rather than the original charter.
- **Urk. 32a (1254):** a separate charter from the original then held in the Geheimes Archiv at Königsberg names “Conradus marscalcus et Bartoldus Clest fratres”. This is independent contemporary evidence for Conradus and Bartoldus as brothers.
- **Urk. 48 (7 April 1269):** Conradus miles dictus Clest states that the village Belitz and associated holdings had been granted to him and his already deceased brother Bertoldus for life, with reversion to Colbatz after Conrad's death. The edition is from the Colbatz monastery matricula. This again explicitly identifies the brother relationship.
- The 1251/1254 documents also place Conradus Clest in the ducal witness network; the 1254 record calls him marshal. This is a substantial documentary biography for this pair, but it is not a genealogical bridge to the later Muttrin family.

**Assessment:** Conradus and Bertoldus are a well-attested sibling pair. The 1289 Urk. 75 witness phrase may refer to another pair/group of Clest brothers, as Sauer and von Ledebur argue, but no source found so far identifies those men as descendants of Conradus/Bertoldus. Conversely, no source proves they were unrelated. Keep both possibilities open and do not merge their families or assign the 1289 Clest to the seal-holder without further evidence.

Source: [Urkundenbuch, Urk. 26, 27, 32a and 48](https://v-kleist.com/FG/urkunden/Urkundenbuch_3.pdf).

#### A0. Critical source criticism: the 1289–1364 bridge is contested

A targeted re-check of the printed primary-source editions and Eberhard Sauer's 1939 critique materially changes the confidence assessment in the first section.

**Urk. 75 (27 January 1289):**
- The document is a grant by Pribislaus, lord of Daber and Belgard, to Bukow Abbey of 200 hides near Persanzig. The witness list calls the witnesses the duke's *ministeriales*. The 1862 Kleist Urkundenbuch prints: “Nicolaus Hakenbeke, Johannes frater suus, Cristoforus cnuth, prissebur, clest fratres ... ministeriales nostri.” It says the original was in the Stettin State Archive, Kloster Bukow Nr. 6; the seal had fallen from its grey linen cords and the parchment was damaged in places. [Urkundenbuch, Urk. 75](https://v-kleist.com/FG/urkunden/Urkundenbuch_3.pdf).
- The 1888 Pommersches Urkundenbuch edition prints the witness phrase with a comma after Clest as well: “Prissebur, Clest, fratres”. It identifies the source as the original in the Stettin State Archive, Kloster Bukow Nr. 6, and likewise notes substantial moisture damage and reconstructed text. [PUB III, Urk. 1489](https://kpbc.umk.pl/Content/23561/PDF/Pomorze_062_05.pdf).
- The Kleist Urkundenbuch editor says the original has a comma between Prissebur and Clest, but not after Clest, and argues that *fratres* therefore refers to both men. Quandt develops this into the hypothesis that Prissebur and Clest were brothers, with Clest the progenitor.
- Von Ledebur's alternative, preserved in Quandt's footnote, reads “Prissebur, [the] Clest brothers”: Prissebur is one witness, and *Clest fratres* a plural group/family. This would not identify the 1289 Clest as a brother of Prissebur.
- Eberhard Sauer (1937 dissertation, published 1939) independently argues that the alternative is possible and that Kratz/Quandt's reading is not proven. Sauer notes that the 1248/49–1284 Conrad/Bertold Clest family appears in the Oder region, while the 1289 entry is in Belgard; the relationship of the 1289 Kleist(s) to Conrad/Bertold cannot be determined. Sauer also stresses the documentary gap between 1289, the 1325 Nycolaus, and 1364 Prissebur. [Sauer extracts, pp. 56 ff.](https://v-kleist.com/FG/Adel_Ostpommern.htm).
- **Assessment:** the 1289 charter proves a witness entry containing Prissebur and Clest and establishes their status as ministeriales of Pribislaus. It does not securely prove that they were two brothers, that the second man's personal name was Klest, or that he was the same man as the seal-owner “Klest de Densin”. The family association's seal argument is corroborative but does not fill the parentage gap. Keep “Klest de Densin as progenitor” as a significant family-history hypothesis, not a demonstrated identity/parent-child link.

**The 1325 Nycolaus Klest lead:**
- A separate charter dated 13 November 1325 names “Nycolao Klest” among witnesses to a boundary settlement involving the Dargun monastery. The printed Mecklenburg/Pomeranian record says the text is from the original in the Hauptarchiv at Schwerin, Dargun collection, with the issuer's seal detached but preserved loose. The charter text itself does not state his residence, status, parents or relationship to the later Muttrin family. [Pommersches Urkundenbuch VI, Urk. 3888](https://kpbc.umk.pl/Content/23455/PDF/Pomorze_062_12.pdf).
- Sauer suggests he may have been a citizen of Kolberg, but that is an interpretation, not wording in the witness list. Sauer treats him as an intervening name-bearing witness and explicitly notes the gap until Prissebur in 1364.
- The Kleist association's Urkundenbuch notes this 1325 man as previously unknown to its older family history. **Assessment: independently documented Kleist-name bearer; no proven connection to the 1289 or 1364 people.** He is a genuine new research lead, not a link to insert into the pedigree.

**Urk. 83 (22 June 1364):**
- The Urkundenbuch prints the grant as dated 22 June 1364 and calls the man “Prissebur Klest ... von Muttrin”, holding the other half of 25 hides at Glyne/Glienke. Its source note is important: the editor used a 1589 transsumpt and several sixteenth-century copies, not the original 1364 parchment. [Urkundenbuch, Urk. 83](https://v-kleist.com/FG/urkunden/Urkundenbuch_3.pdf).
- The current online “Wichtige Urkunden” summary mistakenly labels Urk. 83 as 1368, whereas the Urkundenbuch text, the document-to-person index and the 2025 Muttrin volume all give 1364. Urk. 84 is the 1368 Dubbeslaff Cleest item. [Online summary](https://v-kleist.com/FG/urkunden/urkunden.htm); [corrected index](https://v-kleist.com/FG/urkundenzuordnung.htm).
- **Assessment:** the 1364 date and the wording identifying Prissebur with the name Klest and Muttrin are consistently supported by the documentary tradition, but the extant printed evidence for Urk. 83 is through a 1589 transsumpt and later copies. It documents a landholder and place association, not his father.

**Updated confidence for the opening bridge:** Urk. 75 → Urk. 83 remains unproven. The 1325 Nycolaus Klest is a separate possible clue, but it does not bridge the gap. The 2025 Muttrin volume itself accurately characterizes the early-person arrangement as a “wahrscheinliche Anordnung” based on contemporary documents and later family records; this qualification must govern how the whole chain is displayed.

#### A1. Quandt's own three-period framework and the 1477 charter

Quandt's methodological introduction (General History, pp. 45–46) should be treated as the controlling source hierarchy for this early pedigree. He divides the genealogy into three periods:
1. **From 1477 onward:** the oldest surviving fief charter gives a “secure family tree”, although he immediately cautions that individual documentary differences and the insertion of several persons still require investigation.
2. **From 1316 to 1477:** several persons bearing the family name appear, but genealogical links exist only for some; the combinations needed to form a pedigree have varying degrees of certainty.
3. **Before 1316:** three men bearing the family name occur, but whether and how they belong to the family, and which other men are ancestors or collateral relatives, is the question to be investigated. The origin is not already established.

This is unusually explicit caution from the principal genealogist himself. It means the full 1289-to-1698 chain must not be presented as having the same status as the documented 1477-and-later tree.

**What Urk. 125 (13 April 1477) actually says:**
- The surviving edition was prepared from a sixteenth-century authenticated copy (auscultated copy) in the Landesarchiv, not from the original 1477 parchment. The edition also notes further transsumpts in the 1546, 1568 and 1621 fief letters.
- The copy lists Bisprow of Muttrin with sons Hennig, Peter and Hans; Dubbeslaff of Damen with five sons; **Pribbeslaff of Muttrin with his sons, the brothers Reymer and Bartes at Tychow**; brothers Jurgen and Peter at Dubberow; Voltze at Vilnow; and Curt with his brothers at Raddatz.
- It records that the Kleists testified before the duke that they descended from an “Oldervader” who had three sons, from whom respectively came the Muttrin/Damen, Tychow/Dubberow and Vilnow/Raddatz lines. This is a valuable contemporary family claim preserved in the 1477 legal instrument. It does not name the Oldervader or independently establish his parentage.
- The wording of the 1477 copy is significant for the Reimer/Bartes dispute: it expressly places them as sons of Pribbeslaff of Muttrin. Quandt later notes that the 1618 genealogy instead places the Tychow line under Hans, and that this later reconstruction is repeated in 1621 and the 1706 compilation. This is a genuine source conflict, not a trivial spelling variant. The original 1477 instrument/copy chain and the later fief genealogies must be compared before choosing a father.
- The 1575 and 1601 general fief letters describe the Muttrin line as descendants of the deceased Prissebur of Muttrin. Quandt says the underlying family “Nachrichtung” (genealogical statement) has survived neither in original nor copy, although its main content can be reconstructed from the later fief documents. This is an important missing source behind the Muttrin ascent.

Sources: [Urk. 125, 1477, in the Urkundenbuch](https://v-kleist.com/FG/urkunden/Urkundenbuch_3.pdf); [Quandt, genealogy method and three periods](https://v-kleist.com/FG/Genealogie/AllgemeineGeschichte.pdf).

**Assessment:** 1477 is the strongest early family-level evidence for the three branches and their then-current membership. It is a documented legal assertion of descent from an unnamed common ancestor, not proof that the unnamed ancestor is the same person as Klest de Densin in 1289. The exact fatherhood of Reymer and Bartes is disputed between the 1477 copy and later family genealogies.

#### B. The Muttrin bridge: 1364 through the fifteenth century

1. **Prissebur of Muttrin, Urk. 83 (1364).** The family association's current charter cross-reference assigns Urk. 83 to Prissebur of Muttrin in 1364 ([document-to-person index](https://v-kleist.com/FG/urkundenzuordnung.htm)). The 2025 Muttrin volume quotes him as “Prißebur, der Klest heißt von Muttrin” and says he was the second son of Klest (M.1). The 1364 document is the dated anchor; the claim that he was Klest's son is a reconstruction from the early family genealogy of 1576 and an older letter, not something the 1364 wording alone proves. **Grade: strong dated person/estate evidence; parentage probable, not primary-record proven.**
2. **Jarßlaff and Henning as sons of Prissebur.** The 2025 Muttrin introduction explicitly attributes these sons to the 1576 family genealogy and an older letter concerning Glineke. The older letter reportedly says Prissebur obtained the property for himself and in the name of his two sons. This is meaningful family-record evidence, but the original letter and the 1576 genealogy need inspection and provenance assessment. **Grade: plausible and specifically sourced to later family records; not yet independently verified.**
3. **The 1389 and 1407 names.** The current document cross-reference assigns a 1389 entry to Pritzbor at Zatkow and identifies Henning of Muttrin in Urk. 100 (2 February 1407). The 2025 Muttrin introduction says the 1407 record names Henning M.8 as the son of Henning M.3; it also discusses Jarßlaff's sons Kurt of Damen and Henning of Bruskow. **Grade: dated documentary appearances are strong; the family relationships must be kept attached to the exact named person and charter, not inferred from similar names.**
4. **Henning M.8 and the seven sons.** The 2025 Muttrin introduction says Henning M.8 was alive in 1435 (Urk. 104), married a von Wedelstett according to the old family genealogy, and had seven sons: Bisprow, Pribslaff, Dubbeschlaff, Jacob, Schir, David and Voltzen. The seven-child list comes from the old genealogy; it is not stated as a full seven-son list in the cited 1435 record. **Grade: mixed evidence; dated life evidence plus later pedigree for wife/children.**
5. **Bisprow and the 1477 enfeoffment.** The 2025 Muttrin volume identifies Bisprow (III.1) as son of Henning M.8, alive in the 1477 record and deceased about 1493. The 1477 collective fief letter is a strong documentary anchor for named landholders and some stated son-groups. It does not by itself prove every earlier link back to Prissebur.

The 2025 Muttrin volume itself says the early arrangement of persons is probable and based on contemporary documents together with later family records. This is a critical qualification, not a minor disclaimer. See [Muttrin, 2025](https://www.v-kleist.com/FG/Muttrin/Muttrin.pdf), introduction (PDF text around pp. 3–7), and the [charter cross-reference](https://v-kleist.com/FG/urkundenzuordnung.htm). Note a date correction for our working notes: **Urk. 83 is indexed as 1364 for Prissebur of Muttrin; 1368 is Urk. 84 for Dubbeslaff Cleest.** Do not carry forward a 1368 date for Prissebur.

#### B1. What the 1407 and 1435 documents actually establish

The original-source editions give a useful distinction between dated appearances and the parentage supplied by later genealogy.

- **Urk. 100, 2 February 1407:** the Urkundenbuch says the original was in the Pommersches Provinzialarchiv, Schr. XIX, Chat. 4, Nr. 65. It names the old Bispraw Clest; Henning Clest of Bruskow; Henning Clest of Raddatz; Henning Clest of Muttrin; Kurt Clest of Damen; Prybbeslaf Clest of Kresyn; and another Prybbeslaf, son of Henning Clest of Raddatz. They jointly guarantee for their cousin Clest, Henning's son of Raddatz, held prisoner at Neustettin. Eight seal straps are noted, two missing, with only an indistinct seal remnant on the last.
- The record establishes a group of named Kleists, their estates/locations and a cousin relationship to the imprisoned Raddatz man. It does **not** state that Henning of Muttrin is the son of the earlier Prissebur, nor does it supply the full Muttrin parent-child structure. That link is supplied by the family genealogy.
- **Urk. 103, c.1435:** a record of damage to the Teutonic Order's lands names Pribbeslaff Clest and Claws Kameke among men raiding Dąbczlaw and other villages. The edition says it comes from a foliant in the Königsberg State Archive. This is a dated appearance of a Pribbeslaff Clest in the regional conflict network; the record does not state his ancestry.
- **Urk. 104, c.1435:** a letter from Duke Bogislaw IX to the Teutonic Grand Master, preserved in the Königsberg State Archive, says the Order's vogt had seized goods from “our man Henningh Klystes sone van mütteryn” (the son of Henning Kleist of Muttrin). The 2025 Muttrin genealogy identifies this Henning as M.8 and gives him as a son of Henning M.3. The document itself confirms the son and the Muttrin association but does not name the father in the way the later pedigree does; the identification with the 1407 Henning and the exact M.3 → M.8 link depends on the combined record sequence and family genealogy.
- **Urk. 104a, 11 October 1436:** names Hermann Kleist as son of Curt Kleist of Damen, an explicit parentage statement in a separate collateral branch. It is useful evidence that the contemporary document book can preserve direct father-child wording, but it does not prove the Muttrin line.

**Assessment:** the 1407 and 1435 records substantially corroborate the existence, geography and chronology of the people used in the Muttrin reconstruction. They do not, on their own, prove every link in the Prissebur → Henning → Henning → Bisprow chain. The distinction between a named person, a stated parent and a pedigree identification must remain visible.

Source: [Urkundenbuch, Urk. 100, 103, 104 and 104a](https://v-kleist.com/FG/urkunden/Urkundenbuch_3.pdf).

#### C. The 1477 charter and the three main lines

Quandt's transcription of the 13 April 1477 collective fief letter (Urk. 125) says the enfeoffed Kleists testified that they descended from an “Oldervater” who had three sons. It then groups named people at Muttrin, Damen, Tychow/Dubberow and Vilnow/Raddatz. The [family association's charter summary](https://v-kleist.com/FG/urkunden/urkunden.htm) likewise says the letter confirms three lines from a common ancestor and his three sons.

This is important evidence for a **late-medieval family structure and shared agnatic tradition**, but it does not name the Oldervater or prove the exact parent-child links proposed by later genealogists. Quandt's transcription also contains a crucial ambiguity for the Tychow line: it reads “Pribbeslaff zu Muttrin mit seinen Söhnen, die Brüder Reymer und Bartes zu Tichow”; the 2025 Tychow-Dubberow genealogy instead places Reimer and Bartes as sons of Hans I.24. Keep that conflict open until the original charter or an authenticated copy is inspected.

#### D. The Damen branch: Dubislaff “Bratta”

The 2025 Damen volume says Dubislaff/Bratta (III.2), on Damen, was the son of Henning M.8 and Margarethe von Wedelstädt; it dates him to 1477 and death about 1482. It says the 13 April 1477 letter names him and five sons (Drews, Curt, Bisprow, Peter and Pribislaff), and that the sons were enfeoffed again in 1486 (Urk. 192). It also recounts his two marriages and assigns three sons to Catharina von Podewils and two to an unnamed von Glasenapp.

Assessment:
- The **five sons** have a particularly useful documentary route through the 1477 and 1486 enfeoffments.
- Dubislaff's **parentage and wives** are presented in the 2025 family genealogy; inspect the cited old genealogy and original fief records before treating all details as independently established.
- The account of his disputed acquisition of portions of Damen is a historical narrative about inheritance, pledges and family settlement, not proof of a direct line to p3472.
- The Damen branch must not be assumed to be the same as the later Borntin line merely because both sit within the wider Muttrin-Damensche lineage.

Source: [Damen volume, 2025](https://www.v-kleist.com/FG/Muttrin/Damen.pdf), especially the Dubislaff/Bratta biography (PDF text around pp. 2–4).

#### H. Overall verdict and research order

**What can presently be retained**
- Medieval Klest/Clest persons are documented in the Pomeranian record tradition; the 1289 witness list and seal argument are a credible origin lead, but the interpretation of the witness relationship is contested.
- Prissebur of Muttrin is documented in the 1364 charter tradition. His parentage and the Jarßlaff/Henning family sequence depend partly on later family records and reconstruction.
- The 1477 fief letter is the key medieval bridge showing the late-medieval family structure and the three-line tradition.
- The 2025 family histories and Harbou give useful, detailed Muttrin/Damen and Tychow-Dubberow branch structures, but the early generations are not uniformly proven by contemporary records.
- Harbou strongly supports Cartz Ulrik as son of Pribislaff III.281 and Esther von Kameke. The identity of p3472 is clear enough to preserve; his parents are not.

**What remains unproven**
- A fully demonstrated biological chain from Klest de Densin (1289) to Pribislaff III.281 (d.1698).
- Independent primary-record corroboration of the published parentage of Pribislaff III.281 (Hans Jürgen III.186 and an unnamed daughter of Achatz III.110), and correction of the missing structured parent edge in the expanded data.
- Independent primary-record corroboration of Anna Esther von Kameke's identity and parents (Jacob von Kameke and Catharina von Koller).
- The Reimer/Bartes father in the Tychow branch.
- The proposed pre-1600 von Woyten and von Stojentin lines.

### Tychow / Reimer and Bartes: unresolved father conflict

Two sources conflict:

## 1575–1618 fief-genealogy audit — the lost Muttrin descent account and later reconstructions (16 November 2026)

### 1. What the 1575 and 1601 records actually establish

Quandt quotes the concluding formula in the whole-family fief letters:

- **1575, Urk. 502:** the persons listed in the Muttrin stem belong to that line “according to their credible account of descent presented to us” and are descendants of the late Prissebur Kleist of Muttrin.
- **1601, Urk. 547:** repeats the same substance, calling the listed persons descendants of the late Prissebur, formerly seated at Muttrin, according to the account of descent submitted to the duke.

This is substantial contemporary legal evidence that, by 1575, the Muttrin branch had submitted a written pedigree/account tracing its descent to Prissebur. It is not the same as having that pedigree in hand: Quandt says the “Nachrichtung” survives neither in original nor copy. Its detailed sequence of intermediate generations therefore cannot be checked directly against the document that the ducal administration saw. The 1575/1601 letters support the branch-level descent claim to Prissebur, but do not by themselves independently verify every father-son link later printed in the family genealogy.

### 2. The 1608 whole-family letter gives a three-stem classification

Quandt quotes the formulae in Urk. 564 (1608):

- first stem: the Muttrin line and its offshoots at Vietzow, Damen and Dargen, described as descendants of Prissebur Kleist of Muttrin;
- second stem: the Dubberow line, described as descendants of Jacob Kleist of Dubberow and Tychow;
- third stem: Vilnow and Raddatz, described as descendants of the elder Voltz Kleist of Vilnow.

This is an important continuity check: the 1608 fief record preserves the same broad three-part descent model as the 1477 “Oldervater with three sons” statement, and explicitly names Prissebur as the Muttrin-stem ancestor. It does not identify Prissebur with the 1289 witness or seal-owner, and it does not restore the lost pedigree submitted in 1575.

A separate 1608 fief letter, Urk. 565, is described by Quandt as tracing the then-current Damen line by named intermediate generations to the 1477 brothers Peter, Bisprow and Churdt, sons of Dubschlaff. Quandt says the lines of the first and fifth brothers had died out. This is a more detailed branch-specific genealogy than the broad formula in Urk. 564; it concerns the Damen branch and must not be misapplied as a direct pedigree for Muttrin proper.

### 3. The 1618 / 1621 / 1706 genealogies introduce a material contradiction

The whole-family letters of **1618 (Urk. 585), 1621 (Urk. 594), and the compilation made in 1706 for the 1665 enfeoffment (Urk. 662)** rearrange the first, Muttrin stem into three sub-lines: Damen, Muttrin–Vietzow, and Ruschitz–Dargen.

Quandt says the 1618 genealogy traces the other two sub-lines of the Muttrin stem through intermediate links from **Bisprow and Pribschlaff**. In the second major stem, called descendants of “Old Georg”, it traces the Dubberow line from Peter of the 1477 letter, but the Great Tychow line from **Reimer and Bartes as sons of Hans**.

That last assertion conflicts with the wording Quandt transcribes from 1477: the 1477 list calls Pribbeslaw of Muttrin the father of “the brothers Reymer and Bartes” at Tychow. The two statements cannot both be literal descriptions of the same immediate father-child relationship unless there is a copying/reading issue, an otherwise unrecorded Hans, or an error in one of the later reconstructions. Do not silently harmonize them.

Quandt's source criticism is especially important here: he says this later “genealogization” in fief letters from 1618 onward was not independently worked out by the ducal chancery, which lacked the necessary information, but was apparently taken from Paul Bolduan's genealogical work. He says Bolduan also first names Georg as progenitor of the Dubberow stem. Bolduan's work was not yet in existence in 1608, so the earlier 1608 formulation must have come from another source. Quandt further notes that the surviving material submitted by the Damen branch for the 1608 enfeoffment (Urk. 563a) does not supply the data needed for the later genealogization; the source of the 1608 information therefore remains unknown.

### 4. Revised evidentiary assessment

| Claim | Current assessment |
|---|---|
| The Muttrin branch claimed descent from Prissebur by 1575 | **Strong, contemporary legal evidence**: explicit formula in Urk. 502, repeated in Urk. 547 |
| A written Muttrin descent account existed in 1575 | **Strong evidence of existence**, but the account itself is lost |
| 1608 grouped Muttrin/Vietzow/Damen/Dargen under Prissebur | **Strong evidence of the contemporary branch classification**, quoted from Urk. 564 by Quandt |
| The Damen branch had a named intermediate pedigree to the 1477 brothers | **Documented as a claim in Urk. 565**, but the full text and source basis still merit direct re-reading |
| Reimer and Bartes were sons of Pribbeslaw, as the 1477 wording says | **Explicit in the surviving 1477 copy as printed by Quandt** |
| Reimer and Bartes were sons of Hans, as the 1618 genealogy says | **Later genealogical claim**, repeated in 1621 and 1706; conflicts with 1477 wording |
| The 1618 genealogy independently proves the correct father | **No**: Quandt attributes the genealogization to Bolduan's compilation, not independent chancery research |
| Prissebur of Muttrin = 1289 Prissebur / Klest de Densin | **Not established by these records** |

The key methodological point: the fief letters are valuable legal evidence that family members represented a descent and were admitted to joint fief rights on that basis. But their genealogical clauses are not all independent corroborations. Later letters repeat earlier family genealogies, and the pedigree document submitted in 1575 is lost. For the Reimer/Bartes question, the 1477 wording remains the earlier statement; the Hans attribution must remain a contradictory later tradition pending inspection of the underlying 1618 source and any Bolduan manuscript or surviving excerpt.

## Genealogy-source stemma: 1555–1614 manuscripts and the Reimer/Bartes conflict (16 November 2026)

### The lost 1555–1560 “Nachrichtung” and Jacob Kleist's 1576 expansion

A closer reading of Quandt's §§ 14–17 materially refines the source history behind the early pedigree:

1. **A Muttrin “Nachrichtung” existed by 1575.** The 1575 and 1601 whole-family fief letters explicitly say the Muttrin stem descended from the late Prissebur of Muttrin according to a written account submitted to the duke (Urk. 502 and 547). The account itself is lost.
2. **Quandt dates that underlying account to c.1555–1560.** He argues that it covered the descendants of Bisprow and derived only the Muttrin stem from Prissebur, whom it placed around 1320. Quandt proposes that its author may have been the learned Pribslaw of Muttrin/Borntin (born c.1486, died 1570), formerly ducal/bishop's chancellor and cathedral scholastic. This authorship and the c.1320 placement are Quandt's reconstruction, not statements preserved in the lost document.
3. **Jacob Kleist of Ruschitz expanded it in 1576.** Jacob was ducal chancellor and had access to the ducal archives, fief records, Marienstift foundation, and Colbatz monastery registers. Quandt identifies him as the author of the 1576 expansion and says he could consult the original and copies of the 1364 fief record during border disputes. This makes the 1576 compilation an unusually informed family source, but it remains a compiled genealogy and needs comparison with the cited documents.
4. **A further redaction was made around 1590.** The surviving witness was copied by Kratz in 1863 from a Ruschitz estate-archive manuscript, later apparently lost. The copy has two mid-17th-century scribal hands and incorporates material from earlier redactions. Quandt distinguishes the 1576 original, a 1590 redaction, and a later c.1660 Ruschitz copy/manuscript. We do not possess the 1576 autograph.
5. **Bolduan used Jacob's 1576 original.** Quandt argues that Paul Bolduan's Schema amplissimae nobilissimaeque familiae Kleistianae, composed in 1613 or 1614, was based on Jacob's 1576 genealogy and selectively carried some branches forward. It is therefore not an independent witness to the 1576 pedigree. The 1618, 1621 and 1706 fief-letter genealogizations appear to have been taken from Bolduan, not independently researched by the ducal chancery.

This source stemma is essential: a 1576 family account, Bolduan's 1613/14 schema, and the 1618/1621/1706 fief genealogies cannot be counted as four independent confirmations where they share a common textual ancestor.

### Reimer and Bartes: the conflict predates Bolduan

The 1477 fief letter, as transcribed by Quandt from the sixteenth-century authenticated copy, names **Pribbeslaw of Muttrin with his sons, the brothers Reymer and Bartes at Tychow** (Urk. 125). By contrast, the family genealogy text preserved in the later Ruschitz/Elzow tradition says that **Hans lived at Tychow and left two sons, Reimer and Bartes**, who received the joint fief after their father's death in 1477. The 1618 and later fief genealogies follow the Hans attribution.

This is not simply “1477 versus a 1618 invention”: the Hans version is present in the later transmission of the 1576/1590 family genealogy and thus may predate Bolduan. But Quandt cautions that the relevant sentences referring to a Stammlinea/Genealogia may be additions either to the 1576 source or to its 1590/1660 redaction; in his view the underlying 1576 narrative author did not know the name of Tessen's father and would otherwise have named him. The 1576 autograph is not extant, so the exact layer in which “Hans” entered cannot now be fixed.

**Assessment:** the contemporaneous 1477 fief text remains the stronger evidence for the immediate father named there: Pribbeslaw of Muttrin. The Hans tradition is an early family-genealogy variant of uncertain textual date, not a reason to overwrite the 1477 statement. Possible explanations include a later insertion, conflation of generations or people, or an error in one of the transmitted texts; none is currently demonstrated. Keep the Reimer/Bartes father unresolved in canonical data and preserve both readings in the research dossier.

### What the 1576 account does and does not say about the medieval origin

Quandt's reconstruction says the lost 1555–1560 Muttrin Nachrichtung traced the Muttrin stem to Prissebur, one of three alleged stem-brothers, and set him around 1320. He explicitly says only the Muttrin stem was derived from Prissebur in that account; the connections of the other two stems to their supposed progenitors were conjectural. This is a much narrower claim than a proven, fully connected three-branch pedigree back to the 13th century.

Jacob's access to the 1364 fief document is a valuable provenance clue: the 1576 compiler may have been able to consult it directly. It does not prove that Prissebur in 1364 was father of a named Henning or that the later 15th–17th-century Muttrin line descends generation by generation from him. Those intermediate links still need their own documentary support.

### Canonical-tree decision

No parent edge is added or changed. The 1477 charter and later family genealogies conflict over the Reimer/Bartes father, and the 1576 autograph plus the lost 1555–1560 Nachrichtung are unavailable. The medieval Prissebur → later Muttrin sequence remains a research-only hypothesis until each intervening link is independently established. The existing canonical p3472 → p1736 (Pribislaff III.281 → Cartz Ulrik III.369) relationship is retained: Harbou's 1897 genealogy explicitly states it, but this audit has not produced a reason to alter it. No new person has been inserted into data/build_data.py.

### Independent re-check against the 2025 Tychow-Dubberow volume

The 2025 second edition of the Tychow-Dubberow biographies makes the Hans attribution explicit: its contents identify “the sons of Hans of Tichow (I.24)” as Reimer and Bartes; the biography says they were first cousins of Jürgen and Peter of Dubberow because their fathers were brothers. It then gives Reimer's wife as a von Born of Grassee and his sons as Bartez, Tessen and Joachim; Bartes's wife as a von Briesen and his children as Martin, Jacob, Andreas and a daughter who married Volze of Raddatz. This provides a more detailed published reconstruction of the Tychow branch and supports the later Hans tradition as the current family-association genealogy. Source: 2025 *Tychow-Dubberower Linie*, pp. 134–136, https://www.v-kleist.com/FG/Dubberow.pdf.

However, the same volume's introduction says that the name Hans in the generation after the earliest period rests on later genealogical material. Its 1477 reference does not quote the father phrase; it cites the charter for Reimer and Bartes being brothers. The actual 1477 text in Urk. 125, as printed by Quandt, separately says “Pribbeslaw of Muttrin with his sons, Reymer and Bartes, brothers at Tychow.” Thus the 2025 volume's Hans genealogy is a published modern synthesis, not independent primary confirmation of Hans's paternity. It appears to preserve the later family-genealogy reading while the 1477 text as transcribed names Pribbeslaw. This reinforces the decision not to change the canonical parentage or present the issue as settled.

The 2025 volume also states that Tessen of Dubberow was a nephew of Hans of Tichow, while his father was allegedly Peter of Dubberow; it explicitly says the family records only state the nephew relationship and identify his marriage and two sons. This helps define the collateral relationship but does not resolve whether Hans or Pribbeslaw fathered Reimer and Bartes.


## Publication assessment — medieval and early-modern Kleist material

The public family tree should include the credible historical evidence and the family-genealogy tradition, but should not draw an unbroken medieval pedigree where the sources do not establish one. The website overview is deliberately a narrative with explicit caveats; it does not create new parent-child edges.

| Claim / person | Public status | What is established | What remains unresolved |
|---|---|---|---|
| Conradus Clest and Bertoldus Clest | **Well established as a sibling pair** | Urk. 27 (1249) explicitly calls them brothers; Urk. 32a (1254) and Urk. 48 (1269) independently support the pair. | No demonstrated connection to the 1289 Belgard witnesses or the later Muttrin stem. |
| 1289 Prissebur / Clest witness entry | **Needs more evidence** | The charter names Prissebur and Clest among the lord's ministeriales. | Punctuation and the scope of *fratres* are disputed; neither a sibling relationship nor identity with the Densin seal-owner is secure. |

| Muttrin descent from Prissebur | **Family genealogy; needs more evidence** | Fief records of 1575 and 1601 report a submitted descent account; Quandt reconstructs a c.1555–1560 account and Jacob Kleist's 1576 expansion. | The original account, 1576 autograph and the intermediate medieval pedigree are not extant/independently verified. The 2025 Tychow-Dubberow volume is useful evidence of the modern family-association reconstruction, but it is not an independent primary witness to the disputed Hans paternity. The 1477 wording and the later Hans tradition should remain visible side by side. The source-stemma analysis and archive retrieval targets above remain active leads; publication does not close the research.


## Kleist line to the Danish Gedde branch: direct chain and next proof targets (16 November 2026)

The latest source comparison makes the Danish connection much clearer. This is the rewarding, well-defined part of the Kleist research and should now take priority over repeating the unresolved Lorenz paternity searches.

### The documented Danish descent

H. W. Harbou's 1897 article, “Slægten von Kleist i Danmark,” gives the following chain:
1. **Pribislaff III.281** of Muttrin, Borntin, Döbel and Drenow, and Esther von Kameke, were the parents of **Cartz Ulrik III.369**. Harbou records his Brandenburg service in Brabant (1692–1694), Danish service from 9 August 1701, promotion to lieutenant-colonel, death at Rendsburg in 1722 and burial on 17 November.
2. Cartz Ulrik married **Barbara Juliane von Kleist**, daughter of Christian Casimir II.82 of the Tychow line. This is a marriage linking the Muttrin and Tychow-Dubberow branches of the same wider family.
3. Their son **Christian Adam III.466** was born 1 October 1705 and baptised at Frelsers Kirke, Christianshavn.
4. Christian Adam's son **Christian Frederik III.601** was born 25 August 1743. He married Anna Margrethe Schubart in 1770.
5. Their daughter **Frederikke Louise Dorthea** was born 5 January 1786 and married **Ove Samuel Gedde** on 24 June 1806.

This is the explicit published bridge from the Kleist family into the Danish Gedde line. Harbou's article cites military, church, probate and official records in its notes, but remains a 1897 synthesis; the cited original records should be checked before describing every link as primary-record proven. The key names, dates and relationship statements are recorded in Harbou, pp. 95–102. Source: https://www.v-kleist.com/FG_allg/Kleist_in_Daenemark.pdf.

### The Muttrin inheritance chain behind Pribislaff III.281

The family association's *Materialzusammenstellung zu den früheren Gütern der Familie v. Kleist*, printed pp. 118–119, supplies a more concrete property-history chain:
- In the second half of the 17th century, Muttrin passed from Hans von Kleist of Borntin (III.186) to his son Pribislaff (III.281).
- Its footnote states that Hans III.186 inherited from his father III.114, who inherited from his father III.54, who inherited from his father III.22.
- After Pribislaff's death, the division agreement dated **12 June 1698** allocated Muttrin to Christoph Jürgen III.470, son of his deceased brother Hans Jacob; Christoph Jürgen then ceded it to his cousin Adam Heinrich III.368, Pribislaff's eldest son.

This is valuable because it ties the numbered pedigree to a succession of named estates and inheritance events, rather than relying only on an online family tree. It is still a compiled estate-history summary, not the original fief or inheritance documents. The 12 June 1698 date here concerns the division of Muttrin; do not conflate it with the separately reported 12 July 1698 Drenow transaction. Those dates need checking against the originals.

Source: *Materialzusammenstellung*, pp. 116–119, especially pp. 118–119: https://www.v-kleist.com/FG_allg/G_Zusammenstellung.pdf. Its introductory note says it is an evolving compilation of 18th- and 19th-century estate descriptions and cross-references the numbered biographies in the family history.

### Where to verify it in the archives

The family association's Greifswald archive inventory gives the most actionable targets:
- **Landesarchiv Greifswald, Rep. 40 Nr. 35 gg, Bl. 2–13:** 99 extracts of Kleist fief documents assembled by Lehnsarchivar Thilo. The family association notes that the original was sold to Peter Christian von Kleist in 1766 and that it is uncertain whether the text now in the archive is the original or a later copy.
- The same file includes genealogical work by Pastor Steinbrück (father, c.1780) and his son (c.1820–1830). These are family-history compilations, not contemporary records.
- **Rep. 41 Nr. 28 Plathe:** the two versions of von der Osten's Adelsspiegel, with handwritten additions. Useful as a separate comparison witness, but not a substitute for the fief documents.

The family association's archive inventory is a secondary finding aid, but it gives precise shelfmarks and describes the provenance caveats: https://www.v-kleist.com/bestand_greifswald.htm. It also identifies Rep. 40 III 35ac and Rep. 40 III 35gg as Steinbrück material. A request should ask staff to identify all extracts concerning III.22, III.54, III.114, III.186, III.281 and III.369, and to distinguish the original charter, contemporary copy, later extract and genealogical annotation.

### Keep the early-medieval uncertainty in its proper place

The 1477 charter conflict over whether Reimer and Bartes were sons of Pribbeslaw of Muttrin or Hans of Tychow concerns the collateral Tychow branch. It does not erase Harbou's later documented marriage link between Barbara Juliane of Tychow and Cartz Ulrik of Muttrin, nor the explicit Danish descent through Christian Adam, Christian Frederik and Frederikke Louise. It does, however, mean that the early Tychow pedigree must remain qualified. The 1364 Prissebur record, the 1477 collective fief letter and the later family genealogies are separate pieces of evidence; none alone proves an unbroken descent from the 13th century to Cartz Ulrik.

**Working conclusion:** the Kleist-to-Gedde connection through Cartz Ulrik and Frederikke Louise Dorthea is a credible, specifically documented published genealogy. The immediate parentage of Cartz Ulrik is supported by Harbou and the family register; the generations behind Pribislaff III.281 are supported by a denser but still secondary property/genealogy trail. The next high-value action is to retrieve the fief extracts and inheritance documents at Greifswald, then compare Harbou's Danish citations with the original church and probate records. No canonical parent edge is changed by this review.

## Kleist–Gedde line: source-critical update (16 November 2026)

Harbou's 1897 account, pp. 100–102, explicitly places Christian Adam III.466 as the son of Cartz Ulrik III.369 and Barbara Juliane von Kleist, and identifies Cartz Ulrik as son of Pribislaff III.281 and Esther von Kameke. Harbou also flags the older *Dansk biografisk Lexikon* IX, p. 219, error that makes Christian Adam the son of his maternal grandfather Christian Casimir. Christian Casimir was Barbara Juliane's father and Christian Adam's maternal grandfather, not his father. Do not restore that mistaken parent edge.

The name should be normalized as **Christian Adam**, while retaining **Christian Adrian** as a historical variant in older German family literature. The von Kleist family archive's online correction reports that a Danish archival investigation established the 1 October 1705 birth date and the Adam form; this remains a reported archival finding until the underlying register entry is inspected.

**Primary-record target — Vor Frelsers, Christianshavn:** Harbou reports baptism on 1 October 1705. Search the parish minister's register FVD 1686–1722 around 1–3 October 1705 (the alternative 3 October date occurs in the older biographical account). Also request the bell-ringer's parallel baptism index, volume 20 (birth index 1704–1822), and follow its reference into the bell-ringer's baptism books, volumes 15–19 or 36. The index/catalogue identifies a search route; it is not itself proof of the parentage. The decisive check is the original baptism entry naming the parents.

**Independent identification of Frederikke:** the Fredensborg Slotssogn baptism of Ove Frederik Christopher Gedde, 24 July 1808 (BSID 594366, image 65), records his parents as Ove Samuel Gedde and “Frederikke Lovise Birthe født Kleist”. Harbou (1897) calls Ove Samuel's wife Frederikke Louise Dorthea. The matching husband, surname and chronology strongly support that this is the same wife, but preserve Birthe/Dorthea as a source variant until the original image and marriage/baptism records are compared. This baptism independently supports her identity as a née Kleist; it does not independently prove her parents.

Harbou gives Christian Frederik III.601's birth as 25 August 1743, his marriage to Anna Margrethe Schubart on 3 December 1770, and daughter Frederikke's birth as 5 January 1786. Rendsburg St. Marien and Christkirche are both candidate baptism series for 1743; neither parish is yet confirmed. Bredstedt is only a first search hypothesis for the 1770 marriage and 1786 baptism, not an established event locality. No individual entry for these events has yet been inspected.

**Current assessment:** the published descent Cartz Ulrik → Christian Adam → Christian Frederik → Frederikke → Ove Samuel Gedde is a credible, well-defined line for public display with an explicit “needs more evidence” status at the primary-register level. The 1808 baptism provides independent evidence for Ove Samuel's wife being née Kleist, but not for her parentage. No canonical relationship or confidence field is changed by this note.
